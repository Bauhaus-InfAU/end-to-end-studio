# Narrative

Reads: brief-reader, analysis, climate
Reads (end): everything
Model: opus

This skill runs twice: first it builds the conceptual framework the project grows from, then it writes the text that explains the finished project. Work in the proposal folder you are given and write only into `narrative/`. Read the brief's checklist (`brief-reader/checklist.md`) and `analysis/`; if one is missing, use the brief's Markdown and the site folder, and say so in `summary.md`. Never invent a fact: every claim names its file and, where there is one, the brief's page. Everything about the competition and the place comes from the brief.

**First run: the framework.** It must be memorable and solve a conflict, and it may be bold and abstract. Before drafting, read the brief's description of the place, its history and the people who live and work there, in full. Answer from the brief and the analysis what the project is for, who will use it and what it should say. Draft three stories in words, each with a different character (the site, its users or an abstract theme): its desire, the conflict in its way, and the transformation the design brings. Where no character comes naturally, use concept, angle and object. Develop each draft in a short paragraph: what it does at the largest scale, what parts the project is made of and what holds each together, one doorstep, and how it grows over time. Then judge the three as a jury would and choose the best prospect: the one that holds together at every scale, not the one with the neatest sentence or the most exact number. A draft strong in one dimension only is a runner-up. Check that the chosen one doesn't restate the brief's own plan if the brief shows one, and add a move of your own that can be drawn and counted. Only now read `climate/`, as limits the chosen story must meet, unless the brief makes climate its central question. The other skills design from this, so keep it clear. Write `narrative.md`:

- **Story:** the three answers, the three drafts, each in its short paragraph, the chosen one and why, and its character, desire, conflict and transformation.
- **Title and subtitle:** short and memorable, not a slogan; the subtitle at most about eight words.
- **Idea:** one clear sentence without jargon, a paragraph on why this site asks for it, and your own move.
- **Principles:** three to five, one line each, that the design skills can check (where green runs, how dense, how people move). A principle may set its own target, marked as one.
- **Storyline:** one section per panel, in the number and format the checklist gives: its message in one line, and the drawings that carry it, named by the existing skills that make them.
- **Jury:** each criterion from the checklist and where the storyline answers it, or "open" or "partly open".

**Second run: the text.** Read every `summary.md` and table in the proposal folder. Write `text.md` for the jury, explaining the finished project in sequence: the idea, the concept, how it was built up (structure, programme, buildings, open space, movement, phases) and how it was approached (what the site showed, what was decided and why). Fit it into the brief's headings, length and language where the brief sets them, and put any other text it asks for, such as a short one for publication, in a file of its own. Give each key figure its source file in a footnote, and leave out figures that don't exist. Leave `narrative.md` as it is: the drawings were made from it. If the design has moved away from the framework, tell it in `text.md` and name the gap in `summary.md`.

**After each run**, write `summary.md` in three lines: the title and the idea in one sentence; the number that matters most to the idea, with its source; what you are least sure of.
