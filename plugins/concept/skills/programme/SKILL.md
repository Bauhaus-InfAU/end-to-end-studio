# Housing & Community Program Skill

Reads: brief-reader

## Purpose
Take the extracted requirements from `brief-reader` in the current proposal folder and define a comprehensive housing distribution (household mix), local market integration, and educational facility (school) space program.

## Instructions
1. Read the brief checklist written by `brief-reader` in the proposal folder you are told, else the newest one. If it is missing, read the brief's Markdown in the project's `brief/`; if a figure is in neither, ask or say so in `summary.md`.
2. Calculate and specify:
**Household Mix & Social Distribution:**
     - ~30% Family apartments (multi-bedroom units located near primary schools and nurseries).
     - ~40% Young couples / starter units (flexible and adaptable layouts).
     - ~30% Shared, student, or co-living housing units (VWG / communal living formats).
**Market & Micro-Economy Facilities:** 
     - Outline ground-floor active plinths, micro-retail units, maker spaces, and community commercial stalls.
**School & Educational Provisions:** 
     - Define spatial capacity, accessibility radii, and program requirements for nursery and primary school amenities.
**Temporal & Multi-Use Programming (Day/Night Shared Uses):**
     - Define dual-use space allocations (e.g., co-working spaces by day converting to community workshop rooms by evening; nursery outdoor courtyards opening as neighbourhood play spaces during non-school hours/weekends).
   
**Urban Agriculture & Circular Infrastructure:**
     - Allocate specific area percentages/metrics for community rooftops, urban gardens, productive green edges, and shared neighbourhood amenities (e.g., tool-sharing libraries, repair cafes).

3. Never invent data: next to every number, its source in the brief or the assumption behind it. No drawings; text and tables only.
4. Write everything into `programme/` in the proposal folder and nothing outside it: tables as CSV, text as Markdown.
5. Create a `summary.md` of exactly three lines: 
   - What you defined (including housing mix and unique spatial strategies).
   - The one key number that matters (total homes provided).
   - The one assumption or uncertainty you're highlighting.
