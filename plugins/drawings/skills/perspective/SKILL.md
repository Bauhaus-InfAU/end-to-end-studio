# Perspective

Reads: massing, streets, open-space, mobility, drawing-graphics, narrative, panel-layout

Inputs: `massing/massing.geojson` in the proposal folder, the project's site folder, `narrative/narrative.md` for what the views should tell, the brief for how many views it asks for, `drawing-graphics/standard.md` for the look, `streets/streets.geojson` for the street surface and `open-space/open-space.geojson` for its trees, when they exist. Never invent data: with no massing, stop and say so; everything else that's missing goes in `assumptions.md`. Work in the proposal folder you're told, else the newest in the project's `proposals/`, else a new `<YYYY-MM-DD>_draft/`, and write only into its `perspective/`.

Run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/perspective.py" <proposal folder> --count N`, with N the number of views you read in the brief, in whatever language it is, or the number of view frames in `panel-layout/layout.json`, each chosen to show what its `shows` asks; without `--count` it makes two and warns. Needs `pip install shapely pyproj numpy requests`, and Chrome or Edge. The site folder is found next to `proposals/` or through the project's `CLAUDE.md`; else pass `--site`. The ground is drawn level, as a two-point perspective needs. `--terrain` draws the real ground instead, from `terrain.csv` (columns `lon, lat, ele`) in the site folder, else from a free open elevation service, sending only coordinates: only for a site whose slope the view must show, since a coarse height grid lifts some buildings' feet above the horizon. The tones come from `drawing-graphics/style.json`. Pass any corrected assumption in a JSON file with `--options`, and under its `style` only a tone to override (the keys are `STYLE` in `scripts/draw_view.py`, line weights and the tones of `views_map` too).

Then check the result; the script can't. Open `views_map.png` and every `_base.png`: does each view show what the narrative needs? A view should read as buildings, public space, landscape and human scale together, with nothing blocking the eye. If not, change the view count or redraw edited cameras with `--views views.json`, and say why in `summary.md`. A view shows whatever the data shows, so read every warning: a warning you can't resolve stays in the summary. Then render: `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/render.py" <proposal folder>` sends each `_base.png` to Gemini with a prompt from the view's reason, the narrative's idea, the style (`render_style` in `style.json`, else the default: a freehand ink sketch over pale flat washes, not a photograph), the render section of `standard.md` (else pass its look as `look` in `--options`), an order to keep the camera, the buildings and the ground and to draw the rest anew (real trees, people doing things, no bicycles, windows and balconies on the drawn floor levels, furniture, planting) one to keep the pink public ground pink and paint the lawns as grass, never flat green, and the colours of `style.json` as the family the picture is coloured from, so it sits beside the plans (each house its own tint and material from that family, lived in, never a row of white blocks), writes `<view>.png` and `render.json`, and warns if a size differs. Needs `pip install google-genai pillow python-dotenv` and `GEMINI_API_KEY` in the project's `.env`, or, with no Gemini key, `REPLICATE_API_TOKEN` there for the same model on Replicate (`--provider replicate`); without a key it stops with one line and no image, so say in `summary.md` that the views are unrendered. The look goes in words: `--references IMAGE ...` sends reference pictures as well, but a whole picture pulls its figures, plants and foreground into the view, so pass them only when the words miss the look, and then name in `summary.md` what the image borrowed. Look at each `<view>.png` once beside its `_base.png` and name in `summary.md` anything the image changed: a building added, moved or gone, a floor more, another horizon, another ground.

The base view draws the buildings as plain blocks with their floor levels and no windows, people or bicycles: the render adds windows and people. Every view is a two-point perspective: verticals vertical, the horizon a third up the picture at eye height, 1.65 m above the level ground every building stands on (with `--terrain`, above the foot of the nearest building in the frame), and the camera turned at least 20° against the walls it shows, never square to a facade or straight down a street. A camera redrawn with `--views` keeps its heading, so turn it yourself. Large, a view is judged on its floors and its edges; small, on its one spatial idea, so keep each view's subject simple. A perspective has no scale: never give one in `summary.md`. The ground takes the plans' tones: the standard's `public` pink on the squares, the shared streets (`mobility/mobility.geojson` routes with `access_only`, 7 m wide) and the park thresholds, drawn over the streets as the plans do; the lawns and parks carry short grass blades, finer with distance, a drawing convention, never on a street or square. Trees are drawn as elevation trees, each at its own crown: a wavy crown in one of five greens of the standard's, shaded on one side, a tapering trunk that forks into two branches inside the crown.

**What it writes:** `view-N_base.svg` and `.png`, `view-N_ids.png` (what each pixel is), `view-N.png` and `render.json` (each prompt and model) when rendered, `view-N_stale.png` for a render whose base a later run redrew otherwise, `views_map.svg` and `.png`, `views.json` (each camera, why it was chosen, its scores), `candidates.csv` (not on a `--views` redraw, which leaves any earlier one as it was), `building_status.csv`, `terrain.csv` if fetched, `assumptions.md`, and `summary.md` in three lines: the views, how many from how many candidates, what you're unsure of. With a massing marked `"standin": true`, or a proposal folder named `test`, the view files and `views_map` start with `TEST_`, the other files keep their names, and the summary says so.
