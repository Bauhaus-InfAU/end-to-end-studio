# Plans

Reads: brief-reader, narrative, massing, streets, open-space, mobility, structure-plan, ground-floor, drawing-graphics, panel-layout

Copy `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/config.example.json` to `plans/config.json` in the proposal folder and fill it from the brief's checklist (where the panels' plan, `panel-layout/layout-plan.json` else `layout.json`, has a frame for a plan, take its `file` name and what its `shows` asks from there; the script draws a framed detail area or site plan at the frame's scale and size itself): the scales of the site plan, the figure-ground and each detail area, whether the site plan shows the storeys, the panel size and margin, and for each detail area the structure plan's centre or neighbourhood it is around (or `phase1`, or a point), with the page of each. Give each detail area its own subject: the first the one the brief names; each further one the district centre or one typical neighbourhood, where the design is densest, never the site's edge, so that no two show the same ground. Read the idea in `narrative/narrative.md` and find the one shape that is the idea, by its name, in a design layer or the site: an area (a green, a corridor) or a line (a valley or an edge: the streets, rail or water it follows), not the places the idea mentions or connects (a square on the strip is not the strip; the centres on a valley are not the valley). Put the narrative's word for it and those names in `idea`; where nothing has the idea as a shape, leave `idea` out. Then run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/plans.py" --proposal <proposal folder> --site <site folder> --config <proposal>/plans/config.json --style <proposal>/drawing-graphics/style.json` (`pip install shapely pyproj`; Chrome or Edge for the PNG). It composites what the design skills wrote: buildings from `massing/`, the street surface from `streets/`, the ground from `open-space/`, stops and hubs from `mobility/`, and in the detail areas only the doors, bike stands, play, bus bays and use labels from `ground-floor/`; a layer that is missing is left out and named in `drawings.json`. If `drawings.json` gives a detail area `overlaps` or `weak` (mostly off the site, or hardly a new building in it), give it another `at` and run once more. If it gives `too_small` (the frame cuts off much of the buildings around its centre), name it in `summary.md` for panel-layout, which owns the frames. A detail area's `not_shown` names what its layers don't give (entrances, play, uses): never draw these yourself, name them in `summary.md` with the skill that should give them. Look at each PNG once. Never fix a colour or line weight here: the look is the standard's.

**What it shows.** Large (a detail area, 1:1,000 and larger): every building with its storeys (roman or arabic, as the standard's `storey_labels` says), entrances where the data has them, each an arrow into its door, the street surface, paths, trees (and a row of street trees along both sides of each street `streets/` draws as a centreline), pools and playgrounds, and names, so a square reads as a place and not as a street: the squares, centres, parks and stops, the streets, and the buildings' special uses (school, daycare, pool); the ground floors open to the public hatched and the shared courtyards marked; at 1:1,000 and larger a ground-floor plan: the outer walls cut in black, open at the doors, the inside white, and each ground floor's use with its area where the layers give it (never a room or a flat the data doesn't hold), so the detail says what the site plan can't. Every plan names its squares, centres, parks and stops. The site plan (1:2,000 to 1:5,000): buildings, storeys if the brief asks, streets, green, trees and water, the detail-area windows outlined, and the narrative's idea under the buildings in the accent's pale tone with its name, a line as a broad band, an area as a tint, so the plan carries what the panels promise. Small (the figure-ground, 1:10,000 and smaller): buildings black on white and the site boundary, filling its panel frame where there is one, centred on the site. The new buildings carry each plan, white with an ink outline and a cast shadow; the accent's pale `public` tone marks the public ground: the massing's squares and mobility's shared streets.

**What to write**, into `plans/`: `site-plan_1-<scale>.svg` and `.png`, `detail-area-<n>_1-<scale>.svg` and `.png`, `figure-ground_1-<scale>.svg` and `.png`, `drawings.json`, and `summary.md` in three lines: each file with its scale on one line (`site-plan_1-3000.svg at 1:3,000 …`); how many of the brief's plans are drawn and fit the panel; what you are unsure of, such as a layer that was missing, what a detail area couldn't show, an idea no layer has as a shape, two detail areas that still overlap or are weak, a detail area too small for its subject, or a plan that had to be cropped.
