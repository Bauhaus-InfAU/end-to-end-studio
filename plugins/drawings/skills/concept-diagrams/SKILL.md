# Concept diagrams

Reads: narrative, structure-plan, phasing, programme, analysis, drawing-graphics, panel-layout

Explain the project's central idea in three to six drawings that together say why the design is what it is. You translate; you don't design. Read the narrative's story, idea, principles and storyline (`narrative/narrative.md`), and the structure plan's GeoJSON and summary: its places, centres and the uses each holds, its phases; use the massing, analysis, climate or mobility only where the idea needs them. With no narrative, draw nothing; with no site, draw schematically; say so.

**First write the argument**, at most six short lines, each with its source: the problem; the conditions that cause it; the central idea; why it answers the problem; the decisions that follow; what they become in space, use and experience; the outcome. Keep only the steps the sources support: a step whose reason you can't find stays out, and `summary.md` names what is missing. Never add a use, height, number, route or decision no source gives.

**Then sort the facts by what they need, and group them into one or two families** (`family`). A fact that needs geography (where things are, what shapes the land, how it is organised or phased) belongs mainly to a *site family*: plans on the same simplified site and turn, one move each, ending with the design's result in its real geometry when the sources have one. A fact that is the idea itself, or what the idea becomes as a place and how people use it, belongs mainly to a *concept family*: a *metaphor* or abstract pictogram, then a section, elevation or axonometric close-up (`focus`) of a real place, with the uses the sources name. A reasoning that adds up can be *layers* (A + B = result). A ratio the proposal's tables give (land, homes, people, trees, sealed ground) can be *pie charts*, up to three side by side, each with its table as source, never an assumed number; what people do in the new places can be an *activity* diagram, a row of vignettes. These are primary purposes, not bans: a family may borrow what helps it read, and a recurring motif gives continuity, but no two drawings explain the same thing (`carries` lists each frame's facts; the script warns, it never deletes). Use one family when all facts are of one kind, never two for variety. Both families share one look: palette by meaning, line weights, labels, notes, captions. A family is drawn and placed together; give frames of one topic across families the same `group`. Number the steps in reading order; a frame that sits elsewhere is titled, not numbered (`number: false`). If `panel-layout/layout.json` has frames for the pictograms, their number and `file` names are fixed: give each the drawing its `shows` asks, in the family that fits it, the type yours where `shows` names only content. Where the concept family has no frame, a frame asking for a concept composition gets one (`compositions`: idea, words and place side by side); without such a frame, make none and name it in `summary.md` as needed and missing. `summary.md` says which type each frame uses and why.

**Draw like a designer, not a map.** One dominant gesture per drawing (the main move in the accent, a thick curved arrow or band); everything else supports it or is left out. Abstract the site (`site_line` dotted or pale, `simplify`, `smooth`, `curve`: the drawing only, never the data), surroundings as soft masses. Colour by meaning from the standard (`color`: what exists pale or in ink, what is kept or protected in its green, water in its blue, the new in the accent), never colour for its own sake. Use people, trees, icons and soft zones (`halo`) only where they show a use, a place or an experience the sources name; they are marked illustrative. Label a word or two; one `note` per drawing may say why in up to eight words. Caption: a title of up to five words and one sentence that says why (because…, so that…), in the narrative's words. Moves acting in turn on one thing are a *sequence* (earlier in ink, the new in the accent, what it removes dashed); what exists before any move is never in the accent. The look comes from `drawing-graphics/style.json`; the spec's `style` only overrides it. Write `spec.json` with, per frame, the argument's step and source it answers (`step`, `source`); render it as `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/spec.md` describes, look at every PNG, and revise until each reads without its caption and nothing overlaps.

Work in the proposal folder you're told, else the newest in the project's `proposals/`, else a new `<YYYY-MM-DD>_draft/`, and write only into its `concept-diagrams/`. Everything outside it, in the proposal or the project, is read-only: never delete, move, rename, overwrite or tidy any of it, whatever its name (other skills' files, logs, `usage.csv`); on a rerun, replace only your own files. Write there: an SVG and a PNG per pictogram, named after its move, `00_sheet` with all of them, `spec.json`, `groups.json`, and `summary.md` in three lines:
1. The pictograms, sequence or separate, each one's drawing type and scale, and what must still read small (usually one panel column).
2. Their number.
3. What's conceptual rather than data, and the one thing you're unsure of.
