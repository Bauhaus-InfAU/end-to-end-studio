# Building types

Reads: programme, massing, open-space, narrative, drawing-graphics, panel-layout

Work in the proposal folder you are told, else the newest in the project's `proposals/`, else a new `proposals/<YYYY-MM-DD>_draft/`, and write only into `building-types/`. The inputs are the programme table (`programme/programme.csv`: use, area, plot, homes, residents, source), the new buildings of the massing (`massing/massing.geojson`: buildings with footprint, `floors`, `height_m`, `use`, `type`), the drawing standard (`drawing-graphics/style.json`) and the drawing's frame (`panel-layout/layout.json`). If the massing or the programme is missing, say so to the user and ask whether to wait for it; if they don't answer or say go on, the script draws a stand-in: generic types of assumed size, or the programme line taken from the massing. It writes STAND-IN in the drawing and in `summary.md`, so nobody takes it for the design. Never invent a number to fill a gap.

**Run it.** Copy `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/config.example.json` to `building-types/config.json` and fill each value from the brief's checklist with its page, or leave it empty. Then run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/building_types.py" --proposal <proposal folder>`, write `building-types/notes.json` for the types in `types.csv` and run it again: per type up to five short notes on what it offers, from the narrative and the programme, each `{"text": ..., "source": "narrative, principle 3", "at": "roof"}` (`at`: roof, yard, ground, street, trees, tower or wall); never a note neither of them gives, never a winner's words. It needs Python 3.11 or newer and nothing else to install; the PNG needs a headless Chrome, Chromium or Edge. The scale comes from this drawing's frame in `panel-layout/layout.json`, else from `config.json`, else 1:500, and the summary says which. Fix the script, never the drawing.

**What it draws.** One ensemble per building `type` among the new buildings (or per `use` for community buildings): the massing's `block` with the most buildings of that type, of median footprint among those, drawn whole with the ground around it and turned so its widest street lies in front. All in one true isometric (30°, true lengths on all three axes) at one scale, from low to high, in rows. The buildings are white (the standard's `new`), their left faces in shade (towards its `shadow`), with their `roof` (green, PV rows, gable at an assumed pitch, flat) and an illustrative window rhythm, shop fronts where `ground_floor` isn't housing. Around them the courtyard and the other ground of the massing and the open space, the street at its width and the trees at their `crown_m` and `height_m`. Three to five notes with leader lines say what the type offers: those of `notes.json` first, then the layers' facts (yard, roof, ground floor, tower, street, trees, programme row), each with its source in brackets. Under each its name, the programme rows it answers with their pages, its buildings, floors, height and floor area, and how many blocks and buildings the type has. The scale bar stands upright on each row, because heights are true length. Colours, line weights and fonts come from `drawing-graphics/style.json`; without it, black on white with one accent. At the frame's scale it draws as many types as the panel's content area (without a layout's format, the frame or the panel width in `config.json`) holds, those with the most buildings first and at most six, in rows, and names the others in the summary (`types.csv` keeps them all); if not even one fits, it says at what scale it would. The planned frame is a first guess: it writes the size it needs to `<drawing>.frame.json`, and `panel-layout`'s second run gives it that frame. Never change the scale to fit a frame.

**Write and report.** The script writes `building-types_<scale>.svg` (scale as `1-500`, print size in mm) and `.png`, `types.csv` (one row per type with its figures and sources), `<drawing>.frame.json` and `summary.md` in three lines. Open the PNG once: types that overlap, or have walls showing through roofs, are mistakes. End by telling the user which files were written and where, and how the drawing looks: how many types, the tallest, the look's source, and every stand-in.
