# Isometric

Reads: site-kit, drawing-graphics, panel-layout
Reads (end): massing, streets, open-space, structure-plan

Draw only what the files hold: the project's site folder, `massing/massing.geojson` once it exists, the brief's scales and panel size, and `drawing-graphics/standard.md` if there. State every assumed size in `summary.md`. Run `python3 ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/isometric.py <site folder> <proposal folder> <scale> <panel width mm>` (`pip install shapely pyproj`), with the district's scale and the panel width, its frame as `--frame W H` where it has one, `--fit` where the brief fixes no scale for the isometric, the close view's scale and frame as `--close 1000 --close-frame W H`, `--centre <name>` when its frame's `shows` names another centre or square, and, if there is a standard, its colours as `--style style.json` (keys in the script). It writes the SVGs, the PNGs from a headless Chrome or Edge, and `summary.md`; fix the script, never the drawing. Without a site folder, draw nothing: say which site files you need (the site outline with its buildings, streets, water, green) and write it in `summary.md`.

**View.** A true isometric from the side that fills its frame best and, of those, where the fewest tall buildings stand in front of the new ones, framing them with about 100 m of city, cut by the frame; then, seen from the same side, the main centre close up (the structure plan's, else the largest square) at 1:1,000 to 1:1,500, the edges fading out. Without a massing: the site with 200 m around it, its boundary in the accent, and say the design is missing.

**What it shows.** New buildings white with shaded walls, an ink outline and floor lines, roofs only where the massing names them, and the massing's squares in the accent's pale `public` tone; existing buildings pale (say how many heights are defaults); the streets skill's surface when it exists, else streets at their width, rail, water, green, and the trees the data has, each at its own crown and height in one of three tones of the standard's green, close up with a scalloped crown shaded on the side away from the light; planting only where a design layer or the narrative proposes it; use labels naming the uses the massing gives (a ground floor's use it allots inside a building of another use marked * as illustrative) and people on the design's squares and streets, illustrative. The new reads first, by its outline and shade; the design's parks and gardens told apart from fields. No arrows; no label, person or tree the data and the narrative don't give. Large (a block or a centre, 1:500 to 1:1500) it shows floor lines, roofs with their panels and parapets, use labels and people; small (the district, 1:2500 and smaller) only the volumes, streets, water, green and trees.

**Scale and look.** Its frame's scale in `panel-layout/layout.json`, showing what its `shows` asks, under its `file` name; without one, the brief's site plan scale (the district) and 1:1,000 in the panel's width (the centre); never a smaller scale to fit: the panels crop it; `--fit` takes the largest scale at which the district and its 100 m still fit the frame; print size in mm. The look comes from the standard; without it, black on white and one accent. Check the PNGs: new buildings floating, or cut in the district, are mistakes.

**Write** into `isometric/` in the proposal folder: `isometric_<scale>.svg` and `.png` (scale as `1-3000`), `isometric_centre_<scale>.svg` and `.png`, and a three-line `summary.md`: what you drew and at which scale, the new buildings and default heights, what you are unsure of.
