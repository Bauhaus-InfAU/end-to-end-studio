# The base standard

How a competition entry's drawings and panels look when nothing else is decided. It holds what good plans and boards share: they're readable at their print size, they have a clear hierarchy, and they use few means. The values are in `base.json`. The narrative and the references change the look. The floors marked **floor** stay whatever they ask.

## Type

- One sans-serif family, two weights (regular and bold). No outline, shadow or condensed type.
- Sizes as printed: labels 9 pt, body 11 pt, subheadings 14 pt, headings 20 pt, the panel title 40 to 60 pt. **Floor:** nothing below 9 pt (12 px, 3.2 mm), source lines and scale bars included.
- Running text left-aligned, ragged right, never justified, never in capitals. Line spacing about 1.35.
- **Floor:** a line holds 45 to 75 characters. A wider text box is set in columns, with a gutter of 6 mm.
- Numbers with their unit and source, in the brief's language and number format.

## Colour

- White paper, near-black ink. Context in light greys: existing buildings, streets, the city around.
- One accent for what the entry is about, usually the new buildings or the idea's layer. Nothing else in that colour.
- Nature in muted tones: greens for parks, meadows and trees, a pale blue for water. Never saturated.
- A ramp from light to dark for values (heat, noise, density), the same ramp in every drawing that shows one.
- At most five colours in one drawing besides the greys, and every colour in the legend.
- **Floor:** ink on paper at least 7:1, accent on paper at least 3:1. Don't tell two things apart by red and green alone.

## Lines

- Weights after ISO 128: thin 0.13 mm (hatching, context), mid 0.25 mm (plots, streets, outlines of new buildings), thick 0.5 mm (site boundary, cut lines in sections).
- Existing work is drawn thinner and lighter than new work. A cut is the heaviest line in a section.
- Hatching only where a fill can't say it, at 45 degrees, with the thin line.

## Every plan and section

- A title with the scale, a graphic scale bar, a north arrow on plans, a legend for every colour and hatch, a source line for the data.
- The north arrow: a circle in outline and one line from its centre to the circle, pointing north. No fill, no letter.
- The scale bar: one thin line that steps up and down, a short tick at every step, the numbers under the ticks and the unit only on the last (0 1 2 3 4 5 m).
- Drawn at the brief's scale and printed at true size. The drawing's width at that scale fits the panel's width between the margins; crop to the area the brief asks for.
- What a drawing shows depends on its scale: at 1:500 and larger the block, its entrances and trees; at 1:5,000 and smaller the district in its city, no single buildings labelled.
- One thing dominates each drawing. Everything else is quieter.

## Diagrams

- The same line weights and palette as the plans, the accent for the one thing each diagram says.
- A row of diagrams shares its frame, scale and stroke, so they read as a sequence.
- One short title each, and no more than about ten words of text inside.

## Panels

- Margins of 15 mm, a grid of columns with 6 mm gutters, every box aligned to it.
- The storyline runs from the top left. Each panel has one main drawing, large.
- Captions sit under their drawings. Empty space is allowed; filling it with enlarged diagrams is not.
- The panel title and the entry's title stay at the same place and size on every panel.

## Renders and perspectives

- Taken from the eye level of a person in the place, with the palette's tones. People and trees at the right scale.
- No lens flare, no sunset sky, no glow. The building is not lit brighter than the street.
