# Site section

Reads: drawing-graphics, brief-reader, panel-layout
Reads (end): massing, open-space, structure-plan, narrative

Inputs: the project's site folder (the site polygon and its buildings, plots, streets, water and rail), the brief's checklist for the scales (where `panel-layout/layout.json` has frames for the cut and the excerpt, their scales, what their `shows` asks and their `file` names), the drawing standard for the look, and at the end `massing/massing.geojson`, `structure-plan/`, `open-space/` and `narrative/narrative.md`. Never invent data; if one is missing, say so in `summary.md`. Work in Python with `shapely`, `pyproj` and `requests`, in a metric projection, and write the SVG directly; make the PNG with a headless Chrome or Edge, giving it absolute paths. No Rhino.

**The section.** Run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/section.py" --site <site folder> --proposal <proposal> --scale <site plan scale> --detail-scale <largest scale> --style <proposal>/drawing-graphics/style.json`, and at the end again with `--massing`. It chooses the line itself (buildings, rail, main streets, water, the place you give with `--at`), takes the ground from the site folder's terrain model, else a terrain CSV, else a free elevation service, and says which in `profile.csv`. If the line it chose crosses nothing of interest, give your own with `--line` and say why in `summary.md`. The script runs the line 200 m past the site at each end (in a frame, as far as the frame is long), looks so that A is on your left, samples the ground every 10 m without vertical exaggeration, cuts the buildings on the line in the strongest tone and stands those up to 80 m behind in elevation on their own ground (where the site has 3D building models with roofs, it cuts the roofs along the line so pitched roofs read as pitched and draws those behind by their roof's outline, and a flat top at the measured height stands only where there is no model; give both counts, from `roofs` in `cut.json`, in `summary.md`), draws the ground behind the cut in bands from far to near so no building behind floats, and on the whole cut the landscape further back as ground lines about 300, 600 and 1,000 m behind, palest farthest, where the terrain reaches, puts trees, grass and people by land use, labels each street, railway and river once, marks the site boundary in the accent, and adds a scale bar, heights above sea level and a key plan. It draws the whole cut at the site plan scale and a 200 m excerpt with floor lines and people at the largest scale; with `--massing` the same on the design, on the same line, new buildings in the standard's `new` tone with an ink outline, and the trees of `massing/` and `open-space/` that reach the line or stand just behind it. If the brief asks for elevations, draw them the same way from the street.

**The design pass tells the story**, as the reference sections do: the areas of `structure-plan/` and `open-space/` as coloured bands in the earth, each named at its foot, people on open ground, and the narrative's principles as notes in the sky, each with a dot at its place and one dotted line through them all. Write `site-section/notes.json`, `[{"text": ..., "zone": ...}]`: each principle in the narrative's words, shortened, and the structure plan's or open space's area it is about, by its name (`zones` in `cut.json` lists those on the line), and pass `--notes` with it. A note whose area isn't on the line is spread along the site with a hollow dot, illustrative: name those in `summary.md`. Where the panels' plan (`panel-layout/layout-plan.json`, else `layout.json`) gives the cut or the excerpt a frame, or you give `--frame W,H` in mm, the drawing fills it and leaves the caption, scale bar and key plan to the panel; `assumptions.md` says when the frame is too low for the cut at its scale.

**The look.** Colours, line weights and fonts come from `drawing-graphics/standard.md`. Without it, black lines on white, greys for cut and elevation, and one accent for the site boundary and what is new. Open every PNG before you finish: labels that overlap, a ground line that is missing, or buildings that float are mistakes.

**What to write**, into `site-section/` in the proposal folder: `site-section_<scale>.svg` and `.png` for the whole cut, `site-section_excerpt_<scale>.svg` and `.png`, `profile.csv`, `cut.json`, `assumptions.md`, and `summary.md` in three lines. Write the scale in a file name as `1-2000`, and give each SVG its width and height in mm at its scale, so it prints true to scale. The summary's three lines: what you drew and at which scales, the height difference along the cut, what you are unsure of.
