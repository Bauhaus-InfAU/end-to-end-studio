# Panel layout

Reads: brief-reader, narrative, structure-plan
Reads (end): everything
Model: opus

Work in the proposal folder you are given, else the newest in the project's `proposals/`, else a new `proposals/<YYYY-MM-DD>_draft/`, never the project's top folder; write only to its `panel-layout/`. Needs Python 3.11+ and Chrome, Edge or Chromium (no other installs). First run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/panels.py" scan <proposal folder>`: it lists every summary, drawing (with its print size and the scale its skill gives), table and text, and the inputs that are missing. Read every `summary.md`, the storyline and title from `narrative/`, and the format from `brief-reader/checklist.md`. If there is no checklist, read the format from the brief itself (where the project's `CLAUDE.md` says, else `brief/`): panel size and count, orientation, file limit, reserved boxes such as a code field, panel numbers, names and titles that must appear, the drawings and figures it asks for, and its language. Read the whole brief for these, not only its general submission rules: a site's own list of drawings, scales and figures often comes later, in the task or an appendix. Cite the PDF page for each, not the printed page number. The look comes from `drawing-graphics/standard.md`: fonts, colours, line weights. Without it, leave `style` out: the script's default is black on white with one accent colour. Never pick a look of your own.

**First run, before the drawings: plan the panels.** Compose each panel as a whole before placing anything: a top band of at most a third of its height (a perspective, diagrams, the text), one dominant drawing across the full width that takes about 55 to 65 % of the content area where a drawing at its scale can (the site plan, a detail area or a close isometric), and the other drawings in bands of frames of one size, on the same columns (thematic plans such as mobility, open space, climate and phases at one scale, 1:10,000 where the brief fixes none, in frames of one size). Open the set with the site plan and its surroundings as the first panel's dominant drawing, unless the brief fixes another order; the storyline decides what each later panel tells. The long section runs across the width at the foot of a panel, in a band about 0.15 of its width high where the panel has room; where the brief fixes no scale for it, plan it at the scale at which its ground, buildings and notes fill most of that band, not at the site plan's, since a taller band holds only empty sky. A diagram's frame is at most a third of the panel's content width, in the first run and every later one. Drawings a skill's `groups.json` puts in one group go side by side in one band of one panel. If the project has reference images of whole panels (where its `CLAUDE.md` says, else `references/`), look at each once and compose as they do: how they split into bands, how large the main drawing is, where the title and the numbers sit; never take their titles, texts or names. For every drawing the brief requires and the storyline needs, write an item in `layout.json` with the `file` its skill will write, its `scale` and its `frame` in mm, and in `shows` what it must show; list each in `required` (a storyline drawing with `"source": "storyline"`) with its `file` and, in `by`, the skill the project's list of skills says makes it (leave `by` out if unsure); give the required drawings their frames first, and size each frame at its scale from the extent of what it shows in the structure plan (the new district, a centre), else of the site. A row of building types holds one block of each type with its notes, at about 1:1,000 where the brief fixes no scale; its frame is a first guess, since the types have no size yet; no frame reaches past the data its drawing can show. Render once: a drawing not made yet prints as a grey labelled frame. Look at the panels as a whole, move frames until they read well, save `layout.json` also as `layout-plan.json`, which no later run changes, and end with `summary.md`. The drawing skills then draw to their frames.

**Second run, with the drawings.** Open the first run's `panel-layout/layout.json` and fill it in as `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/layout.md` describes: don't read the brief again or rebuild its format, reserved boxes, marks, `required` or frames (`layout-plan.json` keeps them; the report names every planned item not placed and every change to a planned drawing). First run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/panels.py" frames <proposal folder>/panel-layout/layout.json`: a drawing that wrote a `<drawing>.frame.json` gets the frame it asks for, at its scale, up to the panel's full width; move its neighbours to make room, to another panel where this one is full, and never change its scale to fit. Keep the other frames, except a frame its drawing can't hold, as the version's log or the drawing's own summary says: give that one the frame its drawing needs at its scale, move its neighbours, keep the hero and the bands, and name the change in `notes`. A dominant drawing that fills less than its planned frame keeps the frame, never shrunk to the drawing: where the brief fixes no scale for it, print it at the larger usual scale that fills the frame without hiding more than a quarter of it (the report then asks its skill to draw it at that scale); where the brief fixes its scale, keep that scale. Run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/panels.py" render <proposal folder>/panel-layout/layout.json --check` (no browser) until its report shows nothing you can fix, then once without `--check`. One panel per step of the storyline, from the large scale to the small; without a storyline, the order the brief lists its drawings in, from the large scale to the small. A plan from `plans/` is the one that goes on a panel; a check plan of the same thing from `massing/` never does. A drawing with a scale goes in a frame at a true scale, never stretched: where the brief fixes its scale, it keeps that scale and the frame crops it; where the brief gives none, set `"scale": "auto"` and the script picks the scale, then crops. Where the brief asks for a graphic scale, set `"scale_bars": "all"` in `format`. Aim the crop at what the brief asks to show; a thematic map (mobility, open space, climate) gets no `crop`, so it stays centred. The report names a required drawing whose frame hides more than 25 % and a drawing drawn at another scale than the one fixed: ask its skill to draw it to its frame, never shrink it. Never drop a planned drawing: if it doesn't fit its frame, take the next usual scale where the brief allows it, else resize or move its neighbours. Leave reserved boxes empty. Each panel says a few things large rather than everything small. No text is smaller than 12 px (9 pt) printed, and no line of text runs wider than about 65 characters: a wide text box is set in columns. The script holds both; don't work around them. Every number on a panel shows its source; take the numbers from the tables and summaries, never make one up. The few the storyline rests on go on as big type (`"type": "number"`), each with its source in one line, not as rows of a table. If two numbers that should agree don't (such as a total and the sum of its phases), the panel shows the number of the skill that designs that thing (the homes per phase of the skill that plans the phases, the sealed share of the skill that lays out the open space), with its source; put the other in `notes` and `summary.md` with the mismatch, and never correct either; the report names `figures` rows with one label and two values until `notes` does. Text belongs to `narrative`: use its words, shortened if needed, not your own.

Read `panel-layout/report.md` and look at every `panel-N.png`. Fix what doesn't fit, what overflows and what overlaps. A drawing that never came stays as its empty frame. Every drawing `issues.md` names as available but not on the panels goes on one, at its scale, unless it still doesn't fit after its neighbours move. A number or statement the brief asks for goes on as a `figures` box or a `number`, from the tables that hold it, with their sources. Before a panel is done, fill space left with drawings from the folder that the storyline can use; never blow up a diagram or add content of your own to fill it. Check again with `--check`; render in full at most three more times. What the brief asks for and nobody made stays listed as missing in the report: don't draw it yourself. `issues.md` is the studio lead's list of what other skills must fix, by skill; fix everything under "Fix in panel-layout" yourself. End with `summary.md` in three lines: the panels made and what they show; the one number that matters (how many of the brief's required items are on the panels); what you're unsure of, and how many issues `issues.md` hands to other skills.
