# Panel layout

Reads: brief-reader, narrative, structure-plan
Reads (end): everything
Model: opus

Work in the proposal folder you are given, else the newest in the project's `proposals/`, else a new `proposals/<YYYY-MM-DD>_draft/`, never the project's top folder; write only to its `panel-layout/`. Needs Python 3.11+ and Chrome, Edge or Chromium (no other installs). First run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/panels.py" scan <proposal folder>`: it lists every summary, drawing (with its print size and the scale its skill gives), table and text, and the inputs that are missing. Read every `summary.md`, the storyline and title from `narrative/`, and the format from `brief-reader/checklist.md`. If there is no checklist, read the format from the brief itself (where the project's `CLAUDE.md` says, else `brief/`): panel size and count, orientation, file limit, reserved boxes such as a code field, panel numbers, names and titles that must appear, the drawings and figures it asks for, and its language. Read the whole brief for these, not only its general submission rules: a site's own list of drawings, scales and figures often comes later, in the task or an appendix. Cite the PDF page for each, not the printed page number. The look comes from `drawing-graphics/standard.md`: fonts, colours, line weights. Without it, leave `style` out: the script's default is black on white with one accent colour. Never pick a look of your own.

**First run, before the drawings: plan the panels.** Each panel has one hero drawing across its full width (the site plan, a detail area or a close isometric), with bands above or below it: the thematic plans (such as mobility, open space, climate, phases) in frames of one size at one scale, the long section across the width, a large frame for the isometric close up. If the project has reference images of whole panels (where its `CLAUDE.md` says, else `references/`), look at each once and compose as they do: how they split into bands, how large the main drawing is, where the title and the numbers sit; never take their titles, texts or names. For every drawing the brief requires and the storyline needs, write an item in `layout.json` with the `file` its skill will write, its `scale` and its `frame` in mm, and in `shows` what it must show; give the required drawings their frames first, and size each frame at its scale from the extent of what it shows in the structure plan (the new district, a centre), else of the site. Render once: a drawing not made yet prints as a grey labelled frame. Look at the panels as a whole, move frames until they read well, save `layout.json` also as `layout-plan.json`, which no later run changes, and end with `summary.md`. The drawing skills then draw to their frames.

**Second run, with the drawings.** Then write `panel-layout/layout.json` as `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/layout.md` describes, keeping the first run's frames (`layout-plan.json`; the report names every planned item not placed), and run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/panels.py" render <proposal folder>/panel-layout/layout.json`. One panel per step of the storyline; without a storyline, the order the brief lists its drawings in, from the large scale to the small. A plan from `plans/` is the one that goes on a panel; a check plan of the same thing from `massing/` never does. A drawing with a scale goes in a frame at a true scale, never stretched: where the brief fixes its scale, it keeps that scale and the frame crops it; where the brief gives none, set `"scale": "auto"` and the script picks the scale, then crops. Where the brief asks for a graphic scale, set `"scale_bars": "all"` in `format`. Aim the crop at what the brief asks to show. Never drop a planned drawing: if it doesn't fit its frame, take the next usual scale where the brief allows it, else resize or move its neighbours. Leave reserved boxes empty. Each panel says a few things large rather than everything small. No text is smaller than 12 px (9 pt) printed, and no line of text runs wider than about 65 characters: a wide text box is set in columns. The script holds both; don't work around them. Every number on a panel shows its source; take the numbers from the tables and summaries, never make one up. The few the storyline rests on go on as big type (`"type": "number"`), each with its source in one line, not as rows of a table. If two numbers that should agree don't (such as a total and the sum of its phases), the panel shows the number of the skill that designs that thing (the homes per phase of the skill that plans the phases, the sealed share of the skill that lays out the open space), with its source; put the other in `notes` and `summary.md` with the mismatch, and never correct either. Text belongs to `narrative`: use its words, shortened if needed, not your own.

Read `panel-layout/report.md` and look at every `panel-N.png`. Fix what doesn't fit, what overflows and what overlaps. A drawing that never came stays as its empty frame. Before a panel is done, fill space left with drawings from the folder that the storyline can use; never blow up a diagram or add content of your own to fill it. Render again, at most three more times. What the brief asks for and nobody made stays listed as missing in the report: don't draw it yourself. End with `summary.md` in three lines: the panels made and what they show; the one number that matters (how many of the brief's required items are on the panels); what you're unsure of.
