# Climate

Reads: pdf-to-md, site-kit

This skill describes the place, then says what that asks of the design. Read the brief's Markdown and pictures for climate or fresh air maps, traffic counts, speeds, noise studies and noise limits, and the project's site folder (`site.geojson`, buildings, streets, rail, plots). Work in the proposal folder you're given and write only into `climate/`. Never invent data: every number carries its source (brief page, dataset, year) or is named as an assumption, and what can't be backed is left out and named in `summary.md`. Each step is a script in `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/`, run as `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/<script>.py" --site <site folder> --out <proposal>/climate`; `--help` explains each. Needs `pip install shapely pyproj numpy requests pillow pymupdf tzdata`, and Chrome or Edge for the PNGs.

1. **Fresh air** (`fresh_air.py`). If the brief has a climate map: `page` extracts it; pick three or more street crossings you can see on the map and in reality (`crossing` gives their coordinates), `fit`, and open `fit-check.png`: the site's streets must lie on the map's streets, about 100 m off is normal for a small map. Read the map's colours and legend (if the brief has none, say so), write `classes.json` with the way the arrows point as you see it, and `trace`. Without a map, `derive` from terrain and say there is no study.
2. **Sun** (`sun.py`): sun positions, shadows at 9, 12 and 15 h on 21 March and 21 December, and annual sun hours and radiation from an EPW weather file, so clouds count. It takes an `.epw` from the site folder if there is one, else a typical year from PVGIS.
3. **Wind** (`wind.py`): ten years of hourly wind, as a rose for the year, winter and summer.
4. **Noise** (`noise.py`): write `noise-sources.json` from the brief: each road with its daily traffic, speed and class, or an emission level from a noise study, each with its source; leave out what has no numbers and name it. Buildings screen the noise. The railway is optional: give its trains per day from the brief, a study or the site folder (in the sources file, or `--rail-trains N --rail-source "..."`); without them it stays off and the maps say so. Never assume train numbers outside a test, and mark a test value ASSUMED.
5. **Heat** (`heat.py`): a heat-island index from what covers the ground (roofs, roads, land use, trees, water) in the site folder; relative, not a temperature.
6. **Rules** (`rules.py`): put the brief's or the country's noise limits in `thresholds.json` with their source; without them it uses DIN 18005 and says so. It writes where not to build homes, where only behind a shielding edge, which corridors to keep open, which way to orient streets, and the wind notes. It also writes `climate.json`, the file for the design skills: the DON'T, CAREFUL and DO zones as GeoJSON polygons with the rule and its reason, and the key numbers.
7. **Drawings** (`draw.py`): the look comes from `drawing-graphics/` in the proposal folder (its `style.json`, else the colours and font named in `standard.md`), else black on white with one accent; check `drawings.txt` says which. Take the scale from the brief's checklist (`--scale`). Every map has a legend, north arrow, scale bar and sources. Open every PNG; fix what overlaps or reads wrong. After writing `summary.md`, run `draw.py --sheet` for `climate_sheet`: all maps on one A3 sheet with the summary.

Then write `climate.md`, one page: for each topic what it shows on this site and which parts it constrains, each line with its file or brief page, then the rules, then sources and licences (Open-Meteo CC BY 4.0, PVGIS © EU, OpenStreetMap ODbL). And `summary.md`, three lines: what you made, the one number that matters most (such as the share of the site too loud for homes), what you are least sure of. To check a design, run `sun.py` and `noise.py` again with `--buildings <its buildings>` into `climate/design/`.
