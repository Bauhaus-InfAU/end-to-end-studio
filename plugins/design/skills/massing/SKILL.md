# Massing

Reads: brief-reader, narrative, structure-plan, programme, analysis, drawing-graphics
Model: opus

Never invent data, and never write a site, a city or a number of this project into the skill or a script it makes: the script reads every number from the inputs. Take the site from the project's `site.geojson` and its layers, the rules of the brief (heights, setbacks, what must stay, the scales) from `brief-reader/`, the idea and its principles from `narrative/`, the urban figure from `structure-plan/`, the homes and areas from `programme/`, and what must stay from `analysis/`. If one is missing, read the brief for it yourself, cite the page, and say so in `summary.md`; take the programme only from the brief, never from a guess. Use `site.geojson` as it is, even where it differs from the brief's plans: what lies inside it is existing, and the mismatch goes in `summary.md`.

**How.** Work in Python with `shapely` and `pyproj` (`pip install shapely pyproj`) in a metric projection, and write WGS84, longitude first. No Rhino.

- **Where to build.** Inside the site, on the structure plan's neighbourhoods, minus its green corridors, the kept green and water, every no-build strip of the brief (setbacks from roads, lines and pipes), the main streets, and 4 m around each existing building you keep. The structure plan's streets become `street` (16 m unless it gives a width), but its lines are a framework: where one follows an existing street, keep that street and draw none beside it, and where one would run over a building you keep, it stops 4 m short of it and a path carries it on. Its corridors and centres become `open-space`. Land the structure plan leaves unplanned stays open.
- **Blocks.** The figure comes from the narrative. In `config.json`, `figure` says per neighbourhood (or `*` for all) the typology (`rows`, `open courtyard`, `courtyard`, `points`, `halls`, or a block made of parts: `perimeter` with closed corners a floor higher, `L` for two L's facing each other, `U`, `ensemble` for an L, a bar and a point that vary from block to block, `town houses` on narrow plots of at most 3 floors, `tower` for a block with a tower on one corner, `hub` for a garage with a tower), the orientation (`route`, the default, for along the main or local route each piece of land lies on, or its long side where that holds more blocks; `long side` of the land; or a bearing in degrees, such as the cold-air flow), the row gap and, if the brief sets them, the block sizes (`block`, which also sets a `roof`); name the principle each comes from. Each neighbourhood mixes two or three typologies, unless the narrative gives a reason for one: `mix` in place of `typology`, as shares (`{"perimeter": 0.5, "L": 0.3, "town houses": 0.2}`) or a list the blocks take in turn; neighbouring blocks differ where they can, and a type of another depth (rows, points or town houses beside courtyard blocks) takes its own rows of blocks at its own depth. Closed corners and U's stop the air, so put them where the narrative wants an edge. Without a `figure`, the density decides: low gives rows of houses, medium an open courtyard block, high and urban courtyard blocks with shops below, commercial halls. Blocks sit on a grid per piece of land, which the routes split (one that ends in the land goes on to its edge as a secondary street), with 10 m greened secondary streets between them; along the sides, buildings are 30 to 50 m long with gaps of 5 to 8 m, so air and people pass, and a part that doesn't fit the land breaks into such bars. Blocks less than a third complete are dropped. Every street is led on to the next street, across the green as a path, or ends at a square, the edge of the built land or the site edge; the ends that still meet nothing are counted as `secondary_dead_ends` in `stats.json`, and the summary names them. Courtyards become `open-space` garden.
- **Uses.** Mixed buildings have commerce on the ground floor and homes above. The community buildings the brief asks for (a hub or bus station, a market, a school, a pool) are listed in `config.json` as `community_buildings` and built on the structure plan's centre whose `holds` names them, with their grounds; one named without an area is built at the assumed `community_gfa_m2`, and `summary.md` says so; a school or pool without such a centre takes the nearest courtyard block; daycares take the housing building nearest the centre and then those furthest from each other, with enough floors for their area.
- **Heights.** One floor more within 40 m of a main route and within 150 m of the centre, one less beside the green, unless the structure plan says otherwise. A profile per neighbourhood in `figure` shapes it further: `to centre`, `from green` or `alternate` rows, within `floors_min` and `floors_max`; name the principle it comes from. Then scale floors per use until the floor area meets the programme, within the brief's height limit; where the brief's homes figure is a maximum (`homes_target_is_minimum` false), the homes never exceed it: floors come off the tallest home buildings one at a time, and `assumptions.md` says how many; if more than half the home buildings end at the cap, `assumptions.md` says the programme doesn't fit, so the structure plan can give more land; the two towers (else the two mixed buildings) nearest the centre rise above it only where the brief allows landmarks. Take 3 m a floor unless the brief says otherwise, and list each assumption, with every default above you used, in `assumptions.md`.
- **Trees and squares.** A street tree about every 12 m along every street in the new neighbourhoods, 2 m beyond its edge and never on a street, path or junction, larger along main streets than secondary ones (`tree_sizes`); spacing and size vary from tree to tree (`tree_vary`), so a street reads as planted, not as a grid; a square on each centre, 60 m on the main centre and 30 m on a local one (`square_main_m`, `square_local_m`), cut back to fit the centre and the land its `holds` need.

**Start from the scripts.** `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/` does all of the above. Copy `config.example.json` to `massing/config.json` and fill in every value from the brief's checklist, each with its page or the reason for an assumption, then run `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/massing.py" --proposal <proposal folder> --site <site folder> --config <proposal folder>/massing/config.json`. Where the proposal has `climate/noise-sources.geojson`, then run `noise_at_buildings.py --proposal ... --site ...` and `massing.py` once more with `--design-noise <proposal folder>/massing/noise-design.geojson`: the shapes stay and the uses follow the noise with the design standing, at each building's loudest facade. From `commerce_db` a building is commerce; from `acoustic_db` it stays homes, marked as needing better acoustic protection, and `summary.md` says how many. Then `draw.py --proposal ... --site ...`. Look at the plan, and change a script only where this brief asks for something it doesn't do, such as a use it doesn't know; say what you changed in `summary.md`.

**What to write.** Everything into `massing/` in the proposal folder:

- `massing.geojson`: every feature with a `kind`. `building` (footprint, `floors`, `height_m`, `use`, `type`, `phase` if known, `existing`, and on new ones `block`, `part`, `roof`, `ground_floor` and, after the noise step, `noise_day_db` and `acoustic_protection`), `street` (`width` in m), `open-space` (`use`: park, square, garden, water) and `tree`.
- `areas.csv`: `use, programme_m2, design_m2, difference_m2, source`, one row per use, one for homes (counted in homes, not m²) and one for public green (land area), so the gap to the programme can be seen at a glance.
- `massing.svg` and `.png`: a check plan at the brief's site plan scale, new buildings toned by height and labelled with their floors, kept buildings in outline. Colours, line weights and fonts come from `drawing-graphics/standard.md`; without it, black lines on white and greys by height. The panel plans and the detail areas are the `plans` skill's (in `plans/`), not this one's.
- `massing_3d.svg` and `.png`: a bird's-eye view of the footprints raised to their heights, with the neighbours, to see where the heights rise before the drawing skills start. It is a check, not a panel drawing.
- `summary.md`: three lines. What you placed, the gross floor area against the programme (and the floor area ratio), what you are unsure of.

Open both pictures before you finish. Other design skills add their own layers in their own files and never change this one. If one of them needs the massing to change, it says so in its `summary.md`, and you run this skill again.
