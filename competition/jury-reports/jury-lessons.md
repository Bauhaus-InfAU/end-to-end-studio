# What juries reward

A digest of ten national jury reports from Europan 18: Germany, Spain, France, Italy, Croatia, Sweden, Netherlands, Norway, Finland and one shared by Switzerland and Austria. They cover 46 sites. The site this studio works on is left out on purpose.

This is a reference for people studying the runs. A run doesn't read this file. Written into `narrative` as instructions, its questions made every test run choose the same idea, so they were taken out. Only its main finding stayed in how `narrative` chooses: the winner holds together at every scale, the runner-up is strong in one dimension. It holds no entry names and no quotes from the reports. Neither may reach a panel.

Juries say no project answers everything. France and Italy both write this down, and winners in the Netherlands, Norway and Finland keep open doubts in their own citations. A story that fails a question isn't out. Choose the story whose gaps you can close by drawing.

## What each award looked like

| Level | What the jury saw |
| --- | --- |
| Winner | The whole thing holds together. Balanced across scales, complete, and strongly on the session's theme. |
| Runner-up | Very good in one dimension, often with a limit the jury names, such as housing only or low density. |
| Special mention | One useful part: a method, a tool, a debate, a building system. The Dutch jury defines it this way, as not developed enough to win but holding valuable elements. In Italy the mentions were chosen as ideas for the city's next workshop. |
| Shortlisted, no award | Often an idea that didn't go far enough, or a good scheme with one structural flaw. Spain leaves half of these with praise only and no reason. |
| Cut early | Unclear, incomplete, generic, a method with no place in it, or competent and conventional. |

A good plan doesn't guarantee a winner. Four of France's 14 sites and one of Spain's 10 got no first prize, some because nothing answered the theme strongly enough.

Breaking the brief earned a special mention in Switzerland, the Netherlands and Norway. It didn't win anywhere in these reports.

## Ten questions for a story

Counts are approximate and give sites out of 46. Higher isn't always more useful: 1 and 2 are the two that best separate entries.

**1. Does it stay specific at every scale?** Can you say what this idea does to the territory, the neighbourhood and one doorstep? This was the clearest difference between winners and the rest. About 24 sites, seven reports. If it only works at one scale, expect a runner-up at best.

**2. Where does the idea become a space?** Name the drawing. Then check the drawing says what the text says, and that plan and views agree with each other. The most common doubt in the reports is a strong analysis, method or toolkit that never takes form. About 25 sites, eight reports.

**3. Is there one move you can see on the plan?** A spine, an axis, a system of parks, one reading of the site that organises the rest. About 11 sites, seven reports. It has to stay legible and fit the place. France's president noted fewer grand gestures this session, a road as the backbone was a doubt in Croatia, and Norway's museum site rewarded a building concept instead.

**4. Is it still this idea if only a third gets built?** Juries favoured frameworks that survive phasing, a smaller budget or a change of programme over a fixed design. One winner was praised because it could be reshaped. About 19 sites, six reports.

**5. Who acts, in what order, with what?** Participation and staged processes count when they are designed into the proposal. In Croatia this is what set the winner apart. About 14 sites, six reports. A process alone falls under question 2: toolkits without space got mentions or were cut.

**6. Is it more than competent?** Switzerland and Austria removed five good entries for lack of innovation. Croatia passed over one of its better housing schemes because its premise didn't become a compelling vision. About 5 sites, but these reports say it outright.

**7. What does it change beyond the site line?** France faulted entries that stayed inside the boundary on seven sites. Spain rewarded reaching out on three. About 13 sites. Check it doesn't depend on land nobody can get.

**8. Would it still make sense in another town?** If yes, it's too generic. Juries marked down methods imposed on a site, references with no anchor, landscape unrelated to the local habitat, and elements out of scale with their surroundings. About 12 sites.

**9. What is daily life here, beyond the headline use?** One cultural or community use didn't convince anyone. Juries wanted housing tied to local work, ground floors with real activity, and defined edges. The Dutch jury returned to undefined edges and ground floors on five of its six sites. About 24 sites.

**10. What is the most doubtful move, and does the idea need it?** Juries asked about cost, access, building physics, parking, water and demolition. About 26 sites. This rarely decides alone: winners in Spain, the Netherlands and Norway kept such doubts. In Finland one failed hard check, such as ground levels ignored, did keep good entries from an award. Open technical questions were forgiven more readily in entries framed as strategies.

## Things that don't separate entries

Don't choose a story because it has these. Most serious entries have them.

- Landscape and water as the structure, about 28 sites. Doubted entries have it too. What counts is landscape that generates the layout. A literal translation of water into design was criticised.
- Reusing what exists, about 27 sites. Avoiding demolition is close to expected this session, whose theme was "re-sourcing". That may not carry over to another session. Juries still accepted removal when it was argued.
- Good graphics. Norway and Finland credited storytelling and images on four winners, so a story that makes people want it does count. But French juries praised the graphics and faulted the argument on about six sites.
- Provocation alone. It got entries shortlisted in Italy and admired in Croatia, without an award. With a legible form it can win, as a megastructure did in St. Gallen.

## Where juries disagree

- Density reads both ways. Switzerland asks how little density is still sustainable. The Netherlands marks down low density for lacking urban intensity. Finland calls one dense scheme balanced and another overdeveloped.
- Boldness too. Norway praises bold statements often. Finland penalises bold water and infrastructure ideas as unrealistic.
- France backed winners the city didn't understand, at two sites. Legible to the client isn't decisive.
- Unresolved parking sinks one entry and is forgiven in another. How the entry frames itself seems to matter.
- First-round support is a poor guide. In Germany and France entries strong in round one fell in round two, and one Swedish winner had only been noted for further review.

## Limits of this digest

- One session, one theme. Reports from earlier sessions would show what holds across themes.
- Reports differ in what they explain. France, Croatia, the Netherlands and Finland say most about why entries failed. Spain gives rejected entries no text. Sweden is almost all praise and shows nothing separating its award levels. Germany contributes one site.
- Helpers read the reports, and the site counts are their tallies plus my adding up, so overlaps are possible. I checked 90 of their quotes against the text. All 90 are there, 86 on the page they gave and 4 on another page.
