# Europan 18, Regensburg: jury report on the entry "Lamellen"

*A district the night air passes through*

The jury read the three panels as images, the plans up close, the entry's explanatory text (`narrative/text.md`), its communication text (`narrative/communication.md`) and its storyline (`narrative/narrative.md`), against the brief (`brief/Regensburg_EN_1.md`). Page numbers are the brief's PDF pages, as marked in its Markdown. There is no brief-reader checklist; the criteria and requirements are read from the brief itself. Where a panel and a text disagree, the panel counts.

## 1. Task and criteria

The task is a new district of 2,800 to 3,400 homes for about 5,000 to 6,100 residents on about 107 ha of farmland north of Regensburg, between the B 16, the railway and Pilsenallee, with commerce, social infrastructure, a district centre and an intermodal mobility point at the planned Wutzlhofen stop, to be built in phases, the first between Chamer Straße and the railway (pp. 27, 30, 33). The brief asks for a district that reduces soil sealing, keeps the fresh-air corridors open and protects homes from noise (pp. 28, 33).

The national jury may judge on seven criteria (p. 14). The brief gives no weights, so the jury sets them:

| Criterion (p. 14) | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship between concept and site | carrying | The site's conflict, noise against fresh air on scarce soil, is named in the brief itself (pp. 24, 28, 33). |
| Relevance of the programme to the brief | carrying | Homes, phase 1 and the mobility point are fixed requirements (pp. 30, 33-34), and the city intends to take phase 1 into land-use planning (p. 3). |
| Integration into an urban process | carrying | The commission follows from phase 1 around the mobility hub (p. 3); the brief asks for phasing and residents per phase (p. 34). |
| Architectural and technical qualities | carrying | Tested hardest here: how the blocks and their uses are organised, and how the hub is built. |
| Relevance to the topic, sustainability and adaptability | contributing | "Re-Sourcing" (p. 10) and the unsealed soil (p. 33) are the brief's frame, but every entry shares them. |
| Connection between functions | contributing | A "district of short distances" (p. 28) around the stop. |
| Innovative nature of the public spaces | supplementary | Open space ranks below the interior; here it carries the idea, so a plan that does not show it still counts against the concept. |

The jury also weighs three unwritten criteria, marked as its own assessments: self-evidence, fit with the place, and whether a political majority could back it (all steps need the city council, p. 3).

## 3. Rounds

One entry cannot be compared with others, so the jury reads the brief's two-stage procedure (p. 14) as hurdles.

**Round 1, local jury: conceptual content and innovation with respect to the topic (p. 14).** Passed. The idea is read from the site and can be told in one sentence: built fields alternate with north-south green strips so that the night air passes, while work uses take the loud edges (panel 1). It ties "Re-Sourcing" (p. 10) to two resources the brief names, the cold air and the unsealed soil (pp. 24, 33). The degree of innovation is questioned, because fields separated by green links are already the structure of the framework plan (Fig. 28, p. 37), which the entry admits (`narrative/narrative.md`). The jury passes it on the clarity of its argument.

**Round 2, national jury: the carrying criteria.** Eliminated.

- *Architectural and technical qualities.* The panels do not show how the blocks work. No plan gives uses or storeys; the open-space plan draws every building in the same grey (panel 2) and the isometric every new building in the same red (panel 3). Where the homes, halls, school, pool and the three daycares stand cannot be read from the panels. No building type, ground floor or section through a block is shown; the noise shield the concept depends on is not computed (`narrative/text.md`).
- *Programme.* Detail area 1, which the city intends to take forward first (p. 3), shows the stop as a dot, the mobility point as a symbol on an empty pale square and the P+R garage as a letter "P" (panel 3). The bus station with six platforms (p. 27), the Kiss+Ride and the garage are not drawn, and none of the entry's six mobility points has a building (`mobility/summary.md`). The food market reaches 658 of about 1,200 m² of sales area. The 2,812 homes clear the minimum by 12 on an assumed 90 m² per home; at 100 m² per home the same floor area would give 2,531, below the minimum.
- *Integration into an urban process.* Phase 1 is counted (1,127 homes, about 2,020 residents, panel 2), but there is no phasing drawing and the later phases are "not yet worked out" (panel 2).

**Round 3, prize selection.** Not reached.

**Control round.** The jury tested bringing the entry back as a Special Mention, which the brief keeps for "innovative ideas or insights, yet not sufficiently suitable for the site" (p. 14). It does not hold. The insight, green strips along the cold-air flow with loud uses as a shield, is sound but close to the framework plan, and the drawn design does not carry it consistently: only 48 of the 173 new buildings run within 15° of the air (`narrative/checks.csv`), and the south-eastern housing field is laid out as long east-west rows across the flow (panel 2). The elimination stands.

## 4. Appraisal

The entry reads the site from its climate. The fresh-air, night-noise and rules maps on panel 1 make the conflict legible: 7.7 % of the site too loud for homes by day, 49.2 % habitable only behind a shield, and a cold-air corridor along Pilsenallee that must stay open (panel 1, from `climate/rules.json`, with DIN 18005 values the entry chose because the brief gives none). The siting follows from this: commerce at the Haslbach seam in the north, where the framework plan also places it (Fig. 28, p. 37), green strips along the corridor and the sewer, housing fields between. The jury appreciates that the entry argues from the place rather than from a form, and that its analysis is openly sourced.

The strengths lie in the large-scale structure. No new building stands in the air corridor (0 m², panel 1), the strips keep the sewer and the high-voltage line free as the brief requires (p. 30, `massing/assumptions.md`), the underpasses are kept for foot and bike, and bus gates prevent a car link between Chamer Straße and Sandgasse (panel 3, p. 28). Car-free neighbourhood streets with garages at the edge answer the brief's "few to no cars" directly (p. 34).

The doubts begin where the concept should become a district. The louvre the title promises is not found in the plan. In the north-west field the blocks stand diagonally, in the south-east as east-west rows; it is the green between the fields, not the buildings, that runs with the air, and the entry concedes this in its own text. Whether the strips are wide enough for the air (a mean of about 57 m and 40 m, `narrative/checks.csv`) is not shown. The shield of halls and blocks is asserted but not demonstrated, while 58.2 % of the home floor area lies in the 55-65 dB(A) band (`narrative/checks.csv`). The interior of the blocks is not shown at all: neither the mix of uses within them, which the brief asks for with flexible ground floors and without use-related blocks (p. 33), nor the household mix (p. 33), nor any building type. A jury cannot judge the architecture of an entry that draws none.

Detail area 1 is the decisive weakness. The brief makes the mobility point "the centrepiece of the mobility strategy" (p. 27) and the first phase the start of the commission (p. 3). At 1:1,000 the entry draws its routes with care, but no bus station, no platform, no garage and no building of the hub. West of the railway, where phase 1 is to begin, the plan shows mostly green and existing houses (panel 3). The two landmark buildings at the stop that the text names cannot be found. The perspectives are grey base views without people, uses or surfaces; view 2 shows mainly a blank wall (panel 3).

The key figures meet the brief on paper: 2,812 homes against 2,800 to 3,400, 1,127 in phase 1 against 1,100, 242,749 m² commerce within 230,000 to 276,000 m², 25.4 ha public green against the 6.04 ha required for 5,033 residents, 8,158 m² of play against 7,550 m² (panel 2, recomputed from pp. 28, 30, 34). The jury notes that the homes depend on an assumed 90 m² per home and clear the minimum by 0.4 %, that the food market misses its size by almost half, that the rain pools hold 3,342 of 7,566 m³ at the entry's own 30 mm design rain (`open-space/areas.csv`), and that the public green appears with two different values in the entry's own documents (253,754 m² and 339,847 m²). The sealing shares (35.5 % fully, 1.9 % partly, 62.6 % unsealed) refer to 109.4 ha on a boundary that includes about 20 ha of existing settlement and leaves out land along the B 16; they cannot be compared with the brief's project site of about 107 ha (p. 27) and are therefore questioned.

On the unwritten criteria (jury's assessment): the idea is self-evident and well told on panel 1; it fits the place, whose cold-air corridors the city's own climate map draws north-south (Fig. 20, p. 31); and a council could readily back a structure so close to its own framework plan. That closeness is also its limit: what the city would gain beyond its framework plan is not shown.

Overall, the entry offers a clear and well-founded reading of the site but stops at the scale of the structure plan. The district, its blocks and the mobility hub that the brief puts first remain undrawn.

## 5. Verdict

No award; eliminated in round 2. Not Winner, not Runner-up, not Special Mention (award levels, p. 3).

## 6. Recommendations for further work

1. Draw detail area 1 at 1:1,000 as a design: the stop and its platform, the bus station with six platforms, Kiss+Ride, the P+R and district garage as buildings, the two landmarks, and the phase 1 blocks between Chamer Straße and the railway.
2. Draw the site plan 1:3,000 with numbers of storeys and uses; split or reframe it if it is wider than the panel.
3. Add the figure-ground 1:15,000 and a detail area 2 (the brief recommends the district centre, p. 33).
4. Show the interior of the blocks: building types, ground floors and their flexible uses, the household mix, and where the school, pool and daycares stand with their outdoor areas.
5. Correct the project boundary to the brief's plans (Figs 25-28, pp. 36-37), place the solar park and the 20 m B 16 setback, and recompute sealing and all shares on the brief's project site.
6. Make the concept hold in the buildings: turn the housing rows that cross the flow, or show why they do not block it; test the strip widths against the air flow.
7. Compute the noise behind the halls and blocks on the design, not the free field, for the 58.2 % of home floor area in the 55-65 dB(A) band.
8. Enlarge the food market to about 1,200 m² of sales area and give the pool its 4,000 m².
9. Fix the floor area per home together with the household mix, so the number of homes no longer rests on one assumption; reconcile the two public green figures.
10. Draw a phasing plan with residents for every phase; render the perspectives with people, uses and surfaces and a graphic scale; set the text as an A4 PDF and add the three communication images.
