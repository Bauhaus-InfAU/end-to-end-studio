# Jury report: "The Kept Field", Regensburg Nord, Europan 18

Read from the three panels (P1–P3, `panel-layout/panel-1..3.png`), the explanatory text and communication text (`narrative/text.md`, `communication.md`), the checklist and the brief. Page numbers are the brief's PDF pages. The jury's own checks are in `jury/check.py`. No results, jury reports or entries of the competition were consulted.

## 1. Task and criteria

The city of Regensburg asks for an urban and landscape concept for about 107 ha of farmland in the north of the city, between the settlement edge, the Haslbach commercial area, the B16 and the Regensburg–Hof railway (p. 27). The district is to hold 2,800 to 3,400 homes for 5,000 to 6,100 residents, with at least 1,100 homes in a first phase between Chamer Straße and the railway (p. 33). It is to carry commerce, social infrastructure and an indoor pool (p. 30) and organise itself around a new intermodal mobility point at the Wutzlhofen stop (p. 27, p. 34). It is to treat unsealed soil as the scarce resource (p. 33).

The brief names its criteria without weighting them. The local jury judges compliance, then conceptual content and innovation against the Europan 18 topic. The national jury may judge seven aspects (p. 14). The jury weights them as follows.

- **Carrying.** The relationship between concept and site. The relevance of the programme to the brief: the number of homes is the city's "priority development objective" and phase 1 "must" hold 1,100 homes (p. 33). Relevance to the topic, sustainable development and adaptability, because the brief puts the handling of unsealed soil at the centre (p. 33). The organisation of the blocks and uses, tested hardest as the interior of a project at this scale.
- **Contributing.** The connection between functions, above all the mobility point as the "centrepiece of the mobility strategy" (p. 27). The potential for integration into a phased urban process (p. 33). The innovative nature of the public spaces.
- **Supplementary.** Architectural and technical qualities. In an ideas competition drawn at 1:1,000 at most, these can only be read as the character of the typologies.
- **Unwritten, as the jury's assessments.** Self-evidence of the idea, fit with the place, and whether a political majority could back it.

## 3. Rounds

One entry cannot be compared with others, so the brief's procedure (p. 14) is followed as a series of hurdles.

**Round 1 (local jury): conceptual content and innovation against the Europan 18 topic.** Passed. The entry states its idea in one line, "build on a third, keep the field whole" (P1). It applies the topic "Re-Sourcing" to the brief's own resource, unsealed soil (p. 33). The figure-ground and the site plan show the idea at once: a compact built figure west of the railway, an open field east of it (P1, P2). In a field of entries the jury would place it on the shortlist for the clarity of its stance.

**Round 2 (national jury), first hurdle: relevance of the programme to the brief.** Eliminated here. The entry holds 2,177 homes against the minimum of 2,800. The brief calls this number the priority objective and states it as a floor (p. 33), and the framework plan treats its areas as minimum and maximum values (p. 28). The shortfall follows directly from the concept. Housing land is 15.1 ha against the framework's 25 ha (p. 30), and 89 of 91 housing buildings already stand at the six-storey limit (`massing/stats.json`). Phase 1 misses its "must" of 1,100 homes by 3. The school sports ground is absent, the pool reaches 40 % of its hall, and the daycares' outdoor play is not placed. The panels show the numbers honestly (P1). That honesty is noted, but it does not close the gap.

**Round 2, further hurdles, read for the record.**
- *Concept and site, mobility point.* Not convincing. The brief fixes the stop at the southern settlement edge (p. 36, Infrastructure Plan, item 1) and asks that routes from homes to the mobility point be kept short (p. 27). The figure runs about 1.2 km north from the stop, and no home lies within 400 m of it (`mobility/stats.json`). The land east of the stop stays field. Detail area 1 (P3) shows a paved square, a one-storey market, a rain pool and two ten-storey slabs. The six-platform bus station, Kiss+Ride, Park+Ride and the district garages the text promises are not drawn.
- *Organisation of blocks and uses (interior).* Not shown. All new buildings carry the same red with a storey number (P2, P3). No plan distinguishes housing, mixed and commercial buildings, ground floors or the social facilities. There is no floor plan, no section and no typology drawing. The "closed courtyard edge" at the stop that the text and communication text describe is not legible; the plan shows freestanding slabs. Commerce is gathered in three works and on the Haslbach yards. The brief asks that use-related block formation be avoided (p. 33).
- *Phased process.* Partly. Residents per phase are given (P1). The phases are drawn only as a pictogram that the entry itself calls conceptual and that does not match the built figure (P1, pictogram 5). Phase 3 consists of commerce only.

**Control round: bringing it back for a special mention.** The jury tested whether the strategic idea alone deserves recognition. In its favour, the jury's recomputation shows that the idea is not refuted by the numbers. At six storeys, 2,800 homes need about 19.4 ha of housing land instead of 15.1. The edge would then grow from 34 to about 38 ha, still about 34 % of the site, so "a third" would hold. The entry names this route itself (`narrative/text.md`, "For the team"). A seventh storey alone gives about 2,540 homes and reaches the 21 m limit (p. 28). Against bringing it back: the panels do not make this case; they show a programme that does not fit. The mobility point, the one element all entries must work out (p. 33), is missing in the drawing. The public spaces offered as the counterpart of density, the stop square and the Chamer crossing, are empty paved areas (P3). The kept field is mostly farmland and not public (`structure-plan/checks.txt`), so the idea gives the residents less than it promises. The entry is not brought back.

## 4. Appraisal

The entry takes up a sentence the city wrote itself. The northern field is to take Regensburg's housing "in a concentrated manner" so that better land in the south can stay farmed (p. 22). It carries this logic one step further: the field should not be used up either. A single drawn edge gathers the new district west of the railway, leaning on the settlement edge, the Haslbach estate and Chamer Straße. East of the railway 54.2 ha remain one connected open piece of farmland, meadows, compensation areas, the retention basin, the solar park and the sports grounds (P2). The figure respects the brief's fixed lines. No building stands on the canal strip, under the 150 kV line or on the solar park, and the kept third-party buildings stay in place (P2; `jury/check.py`). The boundary drawn corresponds to the planning area of the brief's plans (p. 36).

The strengths lie in this legibility and in the climate logic. The rows are turned to 188° so that streets and buildings run with the cold night air towards the city (P2, P3; `text.md`), which answers the brief's request to orient buildings and greens to the fresh air corridors (p. 33). Fourteen shallow rain pools on the low ground form a readable blue-green chain through the housing (P2, P3). The figure-ground on P1 makes the gain in open land visible at the scale of the city. The jury acknowledges that the entry states its shortfalls openly instead of hiding them behind claimed figures.

The doubts begin where the idea has to become a district. The housing is a single type: rows about 12 m deep, six storeys, 18 m apart, repeated across all four neighbourhoods (P2, P3). The courts between them, as wide as the buildings are high, are the only shared open space the residents get. Private open space is not shown. The brief asks for varied and flexible development units and a broad range of housing forms (p. 33). Neither can be read; the housing mix is missing entirely. Because the uses are not distinguished in any drawing, the "district of short distances" (p. 28) cannot be verified. The text promises 27 mixed buildings with flexible ground floors. The jury cannot see them on the panels and, trusting the drawing over the caption, counts them as not shown. The noise shield along the railway and Pilsenallee that the brief describes (p. 28) is replaced by open rows with 6 m gaps, and their noise is not assessed (`text.md`, team notes).

The mobility point fails to fully convince. The brief places the stop at the southern edge and makes it the centrepiece. The entry builds the main mass away from it, leaves the land east of the stop unbuilt, and draws the square without its bus station or parking facilities (P3). A district that is "planned with few to no cars" (p. 34) but places 91 % of its housing floor area more than 800 m from its rail stop relies on the buses along Chamer Straße. This the panels do not show either.

The perspectives do not represent the project. View 1 shows a crenellated wall building, and View 2 a single concrete volume on an empty square (P2). Neither is the six-storey row district of the plans, and the entry's own record states that the renders changed the geometry (`perspective/summary.md`). The isometric (P3) alone conveys the three-dimensional figure, and it does so soberly and correctly. Panels 1 and 3 leave their lower fifth empty, while drawings the jury would have needed, such as a phasing plan, a mobility plan, uses and sections, were left in the folder.

The key figures decide the judgement. 2,177 homes and 3,887 residents correspond to 78 % of the framework plan's minimum. The density on the housing land, about 144 homes per ha against the framework's 112, is already higher than the city's plan, so the shortfall cannot be blamed on timidity. It comes from the land the concept allows itself. Commerce reaches 229,195 m², 805 m² short, but 46 % of it is new halls on the yards of the existing Haslbach estate. That estate belongs to third parties who are to be kept and integrated (p. 30, p. 36), and one hall stands closer to the B16 than the 20 m allowed (p. 28). Sealing is stated clearly at 28.9 % fully and 2.4 % partly sealed (P3). These shares refer to 112.8 ha rather than the brief's 107 ha, and the low share is bought partly with programme that was not built. The public green is given as 10.1 ha in the text, 11.4 ha in the massing and 51.9 ha in the open-space count. The jury takes the lowest, which still exceeds the 6 to 7.3 ha required.

Assessment of the unwritten criteria. The idea is self-evident: one look at P1 explains it. Its fit with the place is ambivalent. It follows the city's reasoning for this site, but turns it partly against itself: every home not built here must be built elsewhere, possibly on the southern farmland the city wanted to spare (p. 22). Whether a political majority could back it is questioned. A council that adopted the framework plan in October 2024 (p. 27) with 2,800 homes, and that names affordable housing as "of crucial importance" (p. 27), would hardly accept a quarter fewer homes in exchange for a field that remains mostly private farmland. The jury considers that the entry could gain support only if it showed the full programme inside a slightly larger edge.

Overall, "The Kept Field" is a clear and honest strategic position that would merit discussion, but it remains a diagram. It does not prove that the programme fits, and it leaves the mobility point and the inner organisation of the district undrawn.

## 5. Verdict

Shortlisted after the first round (local jury). Eliminated in the second round (national jury) at the hurdle of programme relevance. Not brought back in the control round. No award: neither winner, runner-up nor special mention (award levels, p. 14).

## 6. Recommendations for further work

1. Fit the programme: show 2,800 homes at least, at six storeys on about 19–20 ha of housing land in an edge of about 38 ha, and state the edge's share of the site again. Show 1,100 homes in phase 1 with a margin.
2. Draw the mobility point in detail area 1: the six-platform bus station with municipal access from Sandgasse and regional access from Chamer Straße, Kiss+Ride, Park+Ride and a district garage, at 1:1,000 (p. 27, p. 34). Consider building the denser part of the figure around the stop instead of 1 km north of it.
3. Show the uses: a plan of ground floors and uses, the mixed buildings, the social facilities and the district centre, so that the district of short distances can be checked.
4. Develop more than one housing type, show the housing mix (p. 33) and at least one typical floor plan or section through a row and its court, with private open space.
5. Place the missing social programme: the school's 3,000 m² sports ground, the pool's 3,000 m² hall on a 4,000 m² plot, and 2,850 m² of daycare play, the last at ground level, not under a ten-storey building.
6. Rework the edge along the railway and Pilsenallee as the noise shield the brief describes, and assess the noise behind it.
7. Clarify Haslbach: which yards belong to whom, how new halls there respect third parties' property (p. 30), and move the hall that stands within 20 m of the B16.
8. Make the field part of the district: say which parts are public, which are farmed, by whom, with what paths and uses, so that the kept land is a public space and not only a reserve.
9. Draw the phasing on the real figure, with pioneer projects and interim uses (p. 34), and add the study-site measures in red.
10. Replace the two renders with views built from the massing that show the rows, the courts and the stop square as designed; use the empty space on panels 1 and 3 for the drawings above. Give one figure for public green and use it everywhere.
11. Submit the text as an A4 PDF with the code box and the three communication JPGs (p. 12).
