# Jury report: Regensburg, "Two Banks"

Europan 18, Regensburg Nord. The jury read one entry: three A1 panels (P1 to P3, `panel-layout/panel-1..3.pdf`) and the explanatory text (`narrative/text.md`). Page numbers are the brief's PDF pages (`Regensburg_EN_1.md`). Where the jury measured something itself, the script and its output are in `jury/check.py` and `jury/check.txt`.

## 1. Task and criteria

The task is a mixed, green and nearly car-free district of 2,800 to 3,400 homes for about 5,000 to 6,100 residents on about 107 ha of farmland north of the city. It is to be built in sections, starting with at least 1,100 homes between Chamer Straße and the railway, with an intermodal mobility point at the new Wutzlhofen stop, a district centre with a food market, three daycares, a primary school, an indoor pool and 230,000 m² of floor area for small firms and crafts (pp. 30, 33–34). The brief asks for varied, flexible buildings, no use-related blocks, flexible ground floors, less sealing, respect for the fresh-air corridors, and shielding of the homes from the noise of Pilsen-Allee and the railway (pp. 28, 33).

The brief lists the national jury's criteria without weighting them (p. 14). The jury weights them as follows:

- **Carrying:** the relationship between concept and site; the relevance of the programme to the brief, read as the organisation of blocks, homes and uses (the brief calls varied, affordable housing its "priority development objective", p. 33); and the connection between different functions, because the brief rejects use-related blocks and asks for mixed ground floors (p. 33).
- **Contributing:** relevance to the topic, sustainability and adaptability (sealing, climate, water); the potential to fit into an urban process adapted to the site (phasing, p. 33); the innovative nature of the public spaces.
- **Supplementary:** architectural and technical qualities. This is a one-stage call for ideas (p. 8), so buildings are judged as types and organisation, not as architecture.

The jury also judges three unwritten criteria, marked as assessments below: self-evidence, fit with the place, and whether a political majority could back it.

## 3. Rounds

**Round 1, local jury: rules, then conceptual content and innovation against the Europan 18 topic (p. 14).** The entry passes. Its idea comes from the site, and the pictograms make it legible (P1, 1 to 6). The railway and Pilsen-Allee lie at the bottom of the valley, where rain and cold air also collect. Instead of the usual noise wall that turns each half's back to the line, work faces the line from both sides "as a town faces its river". This reads the land too loud for homes as a resource, which answers "Re-Sourcing" directly. The jury puts the entry on the shortlist.

**Round 2, national jury (p. 14), in hurdles following the jury's weighting.**

- *Hurdle 1, concept and site (carrying):* passed. The valley section (P1, today and design, 1:3,000) and the edges-and-crossings map (P1, 1:15,000) prove the reading. The brief's air corridor is kept free over 4.49 ha. The longest stretch of line without a crossing falls from 724 m to 395 m.
- *Hurdle 2, programme against the brief (carrying, numbers):* passed. Homes, residents, phase 1, commerce, school, pool, daycares, market and bus station all fall within the brief's figures (Preliminary check). This is the entry's clearest gain over the earlier state of the work.
- *Hurdle 3, organisation of blocks, homes and uses, and the connection between functions (carrying):* **eliminated.** The entry sorts its uses into bands. In the entry's massing, 149 of the 253 new buildings are housing only, 75 are commerce only and 16 mix the two. Not one of the 149 housing buildings on the slopes has a ground floor for anything but homes, while the brief asks to avoid use-related blocks and to plan flexible ground floors (p. 33). The text says so itself: "the slopes are given to homes". No panel shows how a home, a block or a ground floor works. The building types (P3, 1:4,000) are thumbnails without plans or flat types, and the housing mix the brief asks for (p. 33) is absent. The core of the idea, the Station Bridge, is not drawn as a bridge (see Appraisal).

**Control round.** The jury tested whether the entry can be brought back. It cannot return to the prize group: the shortfall is in the carrying criterion the brief calls its priority (housing, p. 33), and it is not a matter of drawing. It is, however, brought back for a Special Mention, which the brief reserves for "innovative ideas or insights, yet not sufficiently suitable for the site" (p. 14). Turning the noise wall round into a working bank, and measuring the idea by the gap between crossings, is an insight the jury considers transferable and worth recognising.

## 4. Appraisal

The siting starts from a precise reading of the ground. The section across the valley (P1) shows the line lying at about 343 m and the slopes rising to about 370 m. The entry draws its order from this: a row of halls and mixed blocks on each bank, ten neighbourhoods of homes behind, and seven green "tributaries" 32 m wide running down to the line with the rain and the cold air (P2, structure plan and open space). The figure respects what the brief protects: the canal strip, the retention basin, the compensation land along the rail, the solar park, the existing stock at Haslbach and Chamer Straße (Fig. 25, p. 36), and the cold-air corridor along Pilsen-Allee, which stays free of buildings. The jury appreciates that the idea is measured rather than asserted. The longest gap between crossings, 724 m today and 395 m with three new crossings (P1), is a figure a city can hold a plan to.

The district centre is placed where the brief wants it, at the Wutzlhofen stop in detail area 1 (P3). The plan shows a bus station with six bays, 300 bike spaces, a market hall, the food market, the school with its yard and sports ground, and daycare 1 on the west bank, with the pool and a district garage on the east bank. Phase 1 carries all of them together with 1,222 homes (P3 table), so the first section works on its own, as the brief asks (p. 33). The idea that "a town starts at its crossing" turns into a credible order of building.

The doubts begin at the bridge itself. The Station Bridge is the entry's centre, its title image and its first phase, yet detail area 1 draws a flat street band across the tracks and Pilsen-Allee (P3). There is no deck level, no ramp, stair or lift, and no platform of the stop. The entry's own files concede that the east ramp does not fit in the space available. The view "from the Station Bridge" (P3) shows a plain square with a blank three-storey façade; it shows neither the bridge nor the line beneath it. The market hall stands beside the deck, not on it. The two further new crossings, at North Head and South Head, appear on the site plan only as thin red lines (P2). What the text promises and the plan does not show is counted as missing, so the crossing, the heart of the idea, remains a diagram.

The jury tests hardest how the blocks and uses are organised, and here the entry fails to convince. The bands that make the idea legible also make it a zoning plan. Work sits in the first row, homes on the slopes, and mixing is confined to 16 buildings and nine centres. The slopes consist of detached L-shaped bars, rows and town houses at three to six floors, set on the cold-air axis (P2 site plan). They form few continuous street fronts, and the courtyards are open on two sides. The panels contain no floor plan, no section through a building, no ground-floor plan of a block and no flat types. The housing mix the brief asks for, from cluster and cross-generational housing to one-room flats and homes for more than four people, is not addressed (p. 33). With 3,237 homes, these are the brief's priority, and the panels say almost nothing about how they are lived in.

Noise protection is questioned. Only 47 % of the west bank and 38 % of the east bank is lined with buildings, against the entry's own target of 70 % (text, "Where the design moved"). The jury's overlay finds homes 54 m from the rail and 72 m from Pilsen-Allee. The entry states that its noise basis leaves out the railway (text, footnote on climate). The claim that no home stands on land at 65 dB(A) or more is therefore not proven for the loudest of the two lines.

The key figures support the programme but not the green ambition. There are 3,237 homes for 5,792 residents and 564,515 m² of floor area: a floor area ratio of 0.50 on the site and 1.31 on the 43.1 ha of new building land. Commerce is 229,475 m², the school, pool and daycares are complete, and phase 1 has 1,222 homes. All of these fall within the brief. Public parks of 26.3 ha far exceed the 6.95 ha required, although a large part of them is the cold-air corridor and land under the power line, space that could not be built on anyway. Sealing is shown on P2 as 39.6 % fully and 2.5 % partly, while the text gives 33.8 % and 15.3 %. Either way, the unsealed share falls from 77.5 % to about 51 %, which the brief's call for less sealing (p. 33) cannot simply absorb. By the entry's own count, only 5 % of the new home floor area lies within a 400 m walk of the stop and 44 % within 800 m (P2). That sits poorly with a district that is to be "nearly car-free" around one crossing. Finally, three halls at Haslbach Edge stand on the B16 and its ramps (p. 28), a conflict the site plan hides by not drawing the ramps.

Compared drawing by drawing with the three awarded entries of this site, the entry falls short mainly in density of information and spatial quality:

- **Site plan.** The first prize, the runner-up and the special mention all make the site plan the hero of their first board, show the whole site and draw continuous blocks, street spaces and a legible green spine. Here the site plan sits on P2, cuts off 1.9 ha, and gives about a third of its frame to the existing Konradsiedlung. Its many small bars do not form streets.
- **Detail areas.** The awarded entries show ground-floor uses, courtyards, paving and furniture. This entry labels uses and areas well, but draws no surfaces, furniture or building interiors, and no bridge.
- **Isometrics.** The awarded isometrics (1:500 to 1:1,000) carry the district's life: roofs, courtyards, people and uses. The 1:5,000 district isometric here leaves about half its frame to empty fields, and the 1:2,000 centre is sparse.
- **Building types.** The awarded entries tie their types to housing models and ownership: cluster housing, assisted living, co-operatives against multiple parties. The type row here is small, at 1:4,000, and says nothing about flats or residents.
- **Perspectives.** The awarded views are peopled and material, with active ground floors. These views are pale and have blank façades.
- **Sections, concept diagrams and phasing.** These are on a par. The valley section is the most exact of all, but the pictogram labels print at about 6 pt.

Self-evidence (assessment): the idea is self-evident in the six pictograms and the section, but not in the site plan, where neither the two banks nor the bridge read without the caption. Fit with the place (assessment): high. The figure follows the valley, the air path and the protected land the brief marks (pp. 30, 33, 36–37). Political majority (assessment): the homes figure, the low heights, land left farmed until its phase, and space for local firms could command broad support. A deck over the railway with no drawn access, halls on a federal road's ramps and a doubling of sealed ground would be hard to defend before the council.

Overall, "Two Banks" offers a convincing, measurable reading of the site and a first phase that works. It does not yet show the district it promises: the bridge, the mixed ground floors and the homes themselves are missing.

## 5. Verdict

**Special Mention.** The entry was shortlisted in round 1, eliminated in round 2 at hurdle 3 (the organisation of blocks, homes and uses), and brought back in the control round for its idea of turning the noise wall into a working bank across a crossable line (brief p. 14). No ranking.

## 6. Recommendations for further work

- Draw the Station Bridge as a bridge. Show the deck level over rail and Pilsen-Allee, the ramps, stairs and lifts to both heads, the platforms of the Wutzlhofen stop, and a section through it at 1:500 or 1:1,000. Put the market on the deck or explain why not.
- Draw the two further new crossings at North Head and South Head with their landings, including those in the study site, highlighted in red (p. 34).
- Break the use bands. Give the slope neighbourhoods flexible ground floors at their streets and corners (p. 33), and show in a ground-floor plan which ground floors take shops, workshops, daycare or common rooms.
- Show the interior: floor plans or a section of each main type, and a housing mix with shares of cluster, cross-generational, one-room and large-family homes (p. 33).
- Move the Haslbach Edge halls at least 20 m from the B16 and its ramps, and draw the ramps on the site plan (p. 28).
- Close the first row toward 70 % of each bank, or show by a noise map that includes the railway that the homes behind it are protected.
- State one sealing figure, on the panel and in the text, with the plots counted the same way. Bring the design back toward the 40 % the entry set itself, for example with fewer sealed courtyards and lighter surfaces at the halls.
- Raise the share of homes near the stop. Put more of the homes above work on the West and North Bank within 400 m, so that more than 5 % of home floor area lies there.
- Fit the 1:3,000 site plan to the whole site, with less of the existing city, and draw the daycare play yards (950 m² each, p. 33).
- Deliver the explanatory text as an A4 PDF with the code box, and the three communication JPGs (p. 12). Set the pictogram labels at 9 pt or more. Make the panel and text figures agree (homes 3,237 or 3,236; gap 389 or 395 m).
