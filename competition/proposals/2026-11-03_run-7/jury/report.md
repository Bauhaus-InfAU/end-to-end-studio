# Jury report: Regensburg, "Furrows: building in rows, keeping the field between"

Europan 18, Regensburg Nord. One entry, read on its three panels (`panel-layout/panel-1.png` to `panel-3.png`), its text (`narrative/text.md`) and its communication text, against the brief (`Regensburg_EN_1.md`, PDF page numbers) and its checklist. Where the text promises something the panels do not draw, the jury treats it as missing.

## 1. Task and criteria

The brief asks for a mixed urban district on about 107 ha of farmland 4 km north of the city centre (p. 27). It is to hold 2,800 to 3,400 homes for 5,000 to 6,100 residents, at least 1,100 of them in a first phase between Chamer Straße and the railway, together with an intermodal mobility point at the new Wutzlhofen stop and a district centre (pp. 30, 33). Soil sealing is to be kept low, open space is to form one network, and fresh air corridors are to steer how buildings and green are laid out (p. 33). The city expressly asks for innovative and bold ideas (p. 33).

The brief lists the national jury's criteria without weights (p. 14). The jury weights them as follows, before judging:

- **Carrying.** *Relevance of the programme to the brief for the site.* The brief calls housing "the priority development objective" and makes 1,100 homes in phase 1 a "must" (p. 33). *Relationship between concept and site.* The site is open farmland on a cold-air path, so the urban figure has to start from it. *Organisation of the blocks and uses.* At this scale this is the interior of the project, and the jury tests it hardest (how the rows, the ground floors, the centre and the mix actually work).
- **Contributing.** *Sustainable development and adaptability* (sealing, climate, soil). *Integration into an urban process* (phasing, pioneer uses). *Connection between functions.* *Innovative nature of the public spaces.*
- **Supplementary.** *Architectural and technical qualities.* This is an ideas competition at 1:3,000 to 1:1,000, and buildings are only expected as volumes.

The jury also judges three unwritten criteria, marked as assessments: self-evidence, fit with the place, and whether a political majority in Regensburg could back the entry.

## 3. Rounds

Because a single entry cannot be compared, the jury runs the brief's own procedure (p. 14) as a series of hurdles.

**Round 1 (local jury): compliance and conceptual content against the Europan 18 topic.** The entry passes. It has one idea and sticks to it: built strips of at most 70 m alternate with 30 m strips of unsealed field, all turned to the north-south cold-air path. That answers Re-Sourcing directly, by treating the farmland as the resource to be kept rather than the land to be used up. The idea reads at once in the isometric (Panel 1) and in the site plan (Panel 2). It would be shortlisted.

**Round 2 (national jury): concept and site, programme, process, public space, connection of functions.** The entry passes on the relation between concept and site and on sustainability. It is eliminated on the relevance of its programme and on the organisation of its uses, both carrying criteria:

- Phase 1 holds 809 homes against a mandatory 1,100 (Panel 2, p. 33). On city-owned land and in a city short of housing, this goes to the core of the task, and the entry concedes that its own rhythm cannot reach the number in this field.
- The mobility point, which the brief makes the focus of detail area 1 and of phase 1 (p. 34), is not drawn. Detail area 1 on Panel 3 shows a wide street space at the stop and largely empty green around it. The deck, the six-platform bus station, the district garages and the food market exist only in the text.
- The district centre does not read as a place. Two 8-storey bars by the station are the only sign of it; no ground floors, no square edge, no market are shown.
- The interior of the district is not shown: no ground-floor uses, no location of school, daycares or pool on any panel, no housing types and no mix. About 69 % of the commerce (157,091 of 227,556 m²) is concentrated in one six-storey hall cluster in the Pilsen loop, which runs against the brief's wish to avoid use-related blocks (p. 33). Craft halls stacked six storeys high are questioned, and the entry questions them too.

**Control round: can the entry be brought back for a special mention?** The jury tests whether the strip principle alone deserves recognition as an innovative idea. It decides not to bring the entry back. What would make the strips more than the green spaces between rows of slabs (they stay farmland, are run by a field cooperative, carry the rain and the cold air) is in the text, not in the drawings: Panels 2 and 3 draw the strips as lawn and meadow, the two views show no field, and the planting, pools and paths repeat a familiar park vocabulary. Without that, north-south rows with green between them are a known type, not a bold one. The elimination in Round 2 stands.

## 4. Appraisal

The entry takes the site for what it mostly still is, a field, and makes its structure the rule of the district. The rows are turned 2° to 9° off north to follow the canal and the cold air towards the city, and every built strip is followed by a strip of unsealed ground 30 m wide. In the isometric on Panel 1 this gives the district a clear, calm grain, which picks up the line of the railway and the existing furrows of the landscape and stays legible from the B16 to Brandlberg. The kept houses on Chamer Straße and at Haslbach, the Coburger Straße workyards, the woods along the B16, the retention basin and the two underpasses are kept and stitched into the figure without force. Work is placed on the loudest edges at Pilsenallee and the Chamer bend, as the brief asks (p. 28), and the climate rules map on Panel 1 shows that this siting was derived, not assumed.

The jury acknowledges how consistently the open-space idea is followed through. The strips, the canal strip, the meadow under the 150 kV line and the corridor along Pilsenallee form one north-south system, and the sealing table on Panel 2 states plainly how much ground stays open. The bars, 13 m deep and set on east-west aspects, promise through-flats with good light on both sides, and the 4 to 6 storeys stay under the 21 m limit throughout. As an assessment, the entry is self-evident: one look at Panel 1 tells what it wants.

The doubts concern what lies inside this figure. The rows are repeated almost without variation across four fields, and the district has no place where the pattern changes to form a centre. Around the Wutzlhofen stop, where the brief expects the densest and most mixed part of the district and the framework plan marks an urban area of high density with Park+Ride on both sides (p. 37), detail area 1 shows green, a road and two taller bars. The mobility hub is reduced to a symbol. School, daycares, pool and market cannot be found on any panel; the jury has to take them from the text. The rows' ground floors are said to be flexible, but no plan shows a single use, entrance or address, and the housing mix the brief asks for (p. 33) is not given. The strips themselves, the heart of the idea, are drawn as lawn and meadow. The working field the title promises is missed, and the two eye-level views, flat colour and a wall of red in View 2, do not convey the space between the rows.

The key figures give a mixed picture. With 2,932 homes and about 5,240 residents the entry sits in the lower part of the brief's range, at a floor area ratio of 1.51 on the 32.9 ha of new building land and 0.44 across the whole 112.8 ha. The unsealed share of 63.4 % is high, but much of it is ground that was never to be built on (the B16 woods, the solar park, the corridor); the strips themselves make up 11.1 ha in the entry's own check, while the text gives 14.1 ha. The 38.5 ha of public green exceed the requirement more than five times, and this generosity is what costs the first phase its homes: 809 instead of at least 1,100. Commerce falls 2,444 m² short of the framework value of 230,000 m² even with every hall at six storeys. The 3,000 m² sports area of the school is missing. These numbers show a principle that was fixed first and not adjusted when the programme did not fit it.

As an assessment, a political majority in Regensburg would welcome the open ground, the climate argument and the low heights, but would hardly back an entry that cannot deliver the first phase on the city's own land and leaves the mobility point undrawn. As a whole, "Furrows" is a clear and well-founded reading of the site whose principle is convincing as a landscape structure, but it fails to fully convince as a district, because its centre, its uses and its first phase are not resolved in the drawings.

## 5. Verdict

**No award.** The entry passes the first round and is eliminated in the second, on the relevance of its programme and the organisation of its uses. The control round does not bring it back for a special mention.

## 6. Recommendations for further work

1. Reach at least 1,100 homes in phase 1 (p. 33): narrow or alternate the strip width in the Chamer–rail field, deepen the bars or add a storey near the station, and show the result in a phasing plan, not only a table.
2. Draw the mobility point in detail area 1 at 1:1,000: the deck over rail and Pilsenallee, the six bus platforms, Kiss+Ride, the district garages and Park+Ride (p. 27, p. 34).
3. Make the district centre a place: break the row pattern around the stop, define a square, place the 1,200 m² food market and the taller buildings, and show the ground-floor uses.
4. Locate and label school, schoolyard, the 3,000 m² school sports area, the three daycares with their outdoor areas and the Nordbad on the site plan (pp. 30, 33–34).
5. Spread the commerce: move part of the 157,091 m² of Pilsen loop halls into mixed rows and smaller workyards for small firms and crafts, and lower the halls to a height crafts can use (pp. 33–34).
6. Show the strips as working field: crops, allotments, the cooperative's yards and the rain pools in plan and section, so that the drawing carries the idea the text claims.
7. Give the housing mix in shares, from one-room flats to cluster and large-family homes, and show how a 13 m bar takes them (p. 33).
8. Place detail area 2 on the panels, at 1:1,000 or 1:500; Panel 2 has about a fifth of its height free for it (p. 34).
9. Replace the base views with rendered perspectives at eye level, at least one of them inside a field strip, and add a graphic scale to the isometric (pp. 11, 34).
10. Draw the study-site measures in red (p. 34), mark the solar park on the site plan, and reconcile the site area (112.8 ha against about 107 ha, p. 27) and the strip area (11.1 ha against 14.1 ha) across panels and text.
11. Deliver the text as an A4 PDF with the code box, and the three communication images (p. 12); remove file names from the panel captions.
