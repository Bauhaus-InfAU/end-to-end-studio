# Jury report: "Field Edge", Regensburg North (Europan 18)

The jury read the three A1 panels (`panel-layout/panel-1..3.png`), the plans close up, the explanatory text (`narrative/text.md`), the storyline (`narrative/narrative.md`), the checklist and the brief. "P1" to "P3" are the panels, and "p." is a PDF page of the brief. Where the text promises something the panels don't show, the jury counts it as missing. The jury recomputed some figures from the entry's own geometry (`jury/_check*.py`); those figures say so.

## 1. Task and criteria

The brief asks for an urban and landscape design for about 107 ha of farmland in the north of Regensburg (p. 27): 2,800 to 3,400 homes for 5,000 to 6,100 residents, at least 1,100 of them in phase 1 between Chamer Straße and the railway (p. 33). It also asks for commerce for small firms and crafts, a district centre with a food market, three daycares, a primary school, the Nordbad pool and an intermodal mobility point at the new Wutzlhofen stop. All of this should be planned with "few to no cars" and a "future-oriented handling of the high value of unsealed soil" (pp. 33–34). Innovative and bold ideas are expressly wanted (p. 33).

The brief lists the national jury's criteria without weights (p. 14). The jury weights them as follows.

- **Carrying.** (a) The relationship between concept and site. The site's main question is soil, noise and cold air, so this criterion decides whether the idea holds. (b) How relevant the programme is to the brief, and how the district is organised inside: blocks, uses, ground floors, the centre. This is the jury's main test. A plan of 3,400 homes stands or falls on how its parts work. (c) How the functions connect, above all at the mobility point. The brief makes that point the focus of the compulsory detail area 1 and of phase 1 (p. 33, p. 34).
- **Contributing.** (d) Sustainable development and adaptability, in line with the session topic. (e) Integration into an urban process, meaning the phasing. (f) Innovative public spaces.
- **Supplementary.** (g) Architectural and technical qualities. In an ideas competition at 1:3,000 and 1:1,000, these show only in outline.

The jury also judges three unwritten criteria and marks them as its own assessments: self-evidence, fit with the place, and whether a political majority could back the plan.

**Admission.** No gap excludes the entry automatically (p. 14: "it has discretion"). The missing text PDF and communication images are delivery gaps; the text itself exists, so the jury reads it. The breaches of the B16 setback and of the solar park are binding rules, but they touch peripheral commercial land and a park overlay, and both can be corrected without changing the idea. Homes and residents exceed the maximum by about 1 %. **The entry is admitted to judging, with these points recorded.**

## 3. Rounds

The brief sets two jury levels (p. 14). The local jury checks the rules and then judges conceptual content and innovation against the session topic, and shortlists at most 25 %. The national jury then names winners, runners-up and special mentions. One entry cannot be ranked against others, so each round is applied as a hurdle.

**Round 1a, the rules: passed with reservation.** See the preliminary check. The B16 setback, the solar park and the 40 extra homes are noted as conditions.

**Round 1b, conceptual content and innovation against "Re-Sourcing": passed.** The entry names the soil as its resource. It draws the site's no-build strips first (noise, the 150 kV line, the canal, the cold-air corridor, the basin) and joins them with the farmland between them into one field of 41.1 ha (P1, pictograms 1–5). That step comes from the site, can be argued, and is bold in the sense of p. 33. The jury would place the entry on the shortlist.

**Round 2, the national jury's criteria: eliminated.** The entry passes (a) concept and site, and (d) sustainability, the second only in part. It is eliminated on (b) programme and interior organisation and (c) connection of functions, both carrying criteria:

- **The interior isn't shown.** Detail area 1 (P3) shows red footprints with storey numbers and nothing else: no legend, no labels, no ground floors, no entrances, no addresses, no square drawn as a square. The 18 mixed blocks with open ground floors (text) can't be found in any drawing. The "open courtyard block" of the type row (P3) is drawn as a plain slab. The 67 courtyard gardens of the text don't show on the site plan, which shows freestanding rows. No household mix is given (p. 33).
- **The mobility point is missing.** The brief makes the intermodal point at the Wutzlhofen stop the focus of detail area 1 and of phase 1 (p. 33). Detail area 1 shows two stop symbols on the track, a wide road and two 10-storey buildings standing in a meadow, but no bus station with six platforms, no Kiss+Ride, no Park+Ride, no district garage, no station bridge and no platform access. The entry's own count puts 2 % of the new housing floor area within a 400 m walk of the stop (P3 mobility legend).
- **The district centre isn't designed.** Station Square, which the text calls the centre with the food market, can't be told apart from a street on P3. Detail area 2 lies largely inside detail area 1, so the second detail adds little that is new and doesn't show the centre either.
- **Commerce misses the framework by 21 %**, and all of it stands in 28 identical 6-storey "halls" of about 6,700 m² (P3 type row). That type doesn't fit crafts and small firms (p. 34).

**Control round, bringing it back as a Special Mention: not awarded.** A Special Mention recognises "innovative ideas or insights, yet not sufficiently suitable for the site" (p. 14). The jury tested whether the idea alone earns one. The innovation is the farmed field as a commons along one built edge. The entry's own plan doesn't show it. The site plan (P2) lays lawn, meadow and park over the field, the text concedes that only 3.9 ha stay farmland of the 23.1 ha the idea keeps farmed, and no field line is drawn on the site plan. The field the pictograms promise (75.6 ha) is not the one the plan delivers (41.1 ha). Crop rows appear only in a rendered perspective (P1). What remains is a sound but familiar green-belt layout, so the idea isn't demonstrated well enough to single it out. The entry stays eliminated.

## 4. Appraisal

The authors start from the site rather than from a picture. The B16 and Pilsenallee noise bands, the protective strips over the canal and under the 150 kV line, the cold-air corridor and the retention basin are not left as scattered leftovers but read together as one open figure. The housing is placed along its edge in eight neighbourhoods on 24.6 ha. The existing Haslbach works and the houses on Chamer Straße are kept, as the settlement structure plan asks (p. 36). The outline of the entry matches the brief's plan, and its traced area of 112.8 ha against the stated 107 ha is accepted as a tracing difference. In its first panel the entry makes this derivation clear and convincing: the five pictograms tell a short, logical story, and the climate rules map grounds it. The jury values that the noise bands carry commerce rather than homes, and that the rows are turned to the 8°/188° axis of the night air flow.

The doubts start where the idea becomes a plan. The "field line" is announced as "one continuous built edge" (P1). The site plan (P2) shows a comb of freestanding 5- and 6-storey rows whose ends meet the field in turning circles. No line is drawn, and no edge reads as continuous. Pictogram 6 claims that phase 1 "draws the whole field line"; since the line is only drawn and not built, it is questioned what holds it in phases 2 and 3. The field itself is shown on the site plan as parkland rather than farmland, and on its eastern edge it covers the solar park, which may not be rezoned (p. 30). The open space thus becomes a generous green belt. That is a quality, but not the farmed commons the title promises.

The jury examined the district's interior most closely. The rows stand about 20 m apart at up to 18 m height; half of the 108 housing buildings stand at the 6-storey cap, so the programme only just fits (`massing/summary.md`). How the homes are reached, how the ground floors along the access streets are used, and where the mixed use the brief asks for actually happens (p. 33, "a use-related formation of blocks should be avoided") is not shown. Housing and commerce are in fact sorted into separate neighbourhoods. The type row (P3) shows five extruded boxes without openings, entrances or floor plans, and the courtyard type has no courtyard. Along the southern boundary of phase 1, 6-storey rows meet the single-family houses of Konradsiedlung directly, without a transition. That is questioned.

The district centre and the mobility point, which the brief places at the heart of phase 1, fail to convince. Station Square appears on P3 as a road junction with two isolated towers. The bus station, the food market and the garages exist only in the text. The jury notes a striking gap in the figures. Measured in a straight line from the rail stop, 42 % of the new housing floor area lies within 400 m (jury's measurement), while the entry's network count gives 2 %. Either the paths don't lead to the stop, or the count is wrong; in both cases the stop isn't the obvious destination of the district. Car access is handled correctly at the five gateways, and the ban on a link between Chamer Straße and Sandgasse is respected. Still, two car streets cross the field, against the entry's own principle.

Key figures. The entry places 3,440 homes for about 6,158 residents, 40 homes above the brief's maximum, at about 140 homes per hectare of housing land and a floor area ratio of 1.55 on the 31 ha of new building land (0.43 across the site). Phase 1 holds 1,315 homes and so exceeds the required 1,100. Public green of 27.3 ha far exceeds the required 7.4 ha, while play, at 7,704 m², falls 1,533 m² short of 1.5 m² per resident. Commerce reaches 180,552 m² against the 230,000 m² of the framework plan. Full sealing rises from 14.2 to 28.2 % of the site, and 60.1 % stays unsealed. The panel also prints a second count of 32.3 / 2.8 / 64.9 % from the open-space plan. The brief asks for one statement (p. 34), and two that contradict each other weaken the very figure the concept rests on.

On the unwritten criteria, as the jury's assessment: the entry is self-evident on panel 1 and loses that clarity on panels 2 and 3, where the field and the line can't be read. Its fit with the place is good in its open-space structure and with the climate, and weaker at the edge with the existing low-rise housing. A political majority seems likely for the principle of keeping a large connected open space and of meeting the housing target. It seems unlikely for a plan that builds on the solar park, falls short on commerce, and leaves the station square, the city's flagship of phase 1, undesigned.

Overall, "Field Edge" makes a clear and well-founded contribution to the question of soil. The jury sees the strength of the entry in its reading of the site, but its plans don't yet carry the idea they announce, and the district's interior, its centre and its mobility point remain undesigned.

## 5. Verdict

**No award.** The entry is admitted and would be shortlisted in the first round. It is eliminated in the second round on the carrying criteria of programme and interior organisation and of the connection of functions at the mobility point. In the control round it is not brought back as a Special Mention, because the plan doesn't demonstrate its innovative core, the farmed field.

## 6. Recommendations for further work

1. Draw detail area 1 as the mobility point. Show the bus station with six platforms, Kiss+Ride, Park+Ride and the district garage as buildings and surfaces, the access to the platforms and the station bridge, and the square with its edges and ground-floor uses, all with a legend and labels.
2. Move detail area 2 so that it no longer overlaps detail area 1, and use it for the district centre or for one neighbourhood at 1:500, with ground-floor plans, entrances, private and shared open space, and the school yard and sports area labelled.
3. Draw the field line on the site plan as what it claims to be, a continuous built and public edge, and show how the rows close towards it.
4. Show the field as farmland where the idea keeps it farmed (B16 Field, Valley Field). Set out the paths and the access for farm vehicles, and remove the park from the solar park.
5. Keep all buildings 20 m from the B16, including its slip roads, and shift the five halls of Haslbach Edge.
6. Bring the homes back to at most 3,400 and the residents to at most 6,100, and add play area to at least 1.5 m² per resident.
7. Close the commerce gap of about 49,000 m² with a type that suits crafts and small firms (high ground floors, yards, mixed rows) rather than 6-storey halls. Distribute mixed ground floors through the housing neighbourhoods as well.
8. Give the housing mix in shares (cluster, cross-generational, one-room, homes for more than four people), and show them in the type row with real floor plans. Draw the courtyard type as a courtyard.
9. State one sealing count, with its method, and draw it as a diagram.
10. Work out the transition at the southern boundary of phase 1 to the Konradsiedlung houses, in height and in setback.
11. Check the path network to the stop, and correct either the network or the 2 % figure.
12. Bring the pictograms in line with the plan (41.1 ha, not 75.6 ha), make all text in the drawings at least 9 pt, and fix the crops (type row, open-space legend, detail area 2).
13. Produce the explanatory text as an A4 PDF with the code box, and the three communication JPGs.
