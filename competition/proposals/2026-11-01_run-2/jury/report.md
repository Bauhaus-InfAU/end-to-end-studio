# Jury report: "Long Field", Regensburg

Europan 18, site Regensburg. One entry, judged on its own against the brief. Sources: the three panels (`panel-layout/panel-1.png` to `panel-3.png`, read as images, the site plan up close), `panel-layout/report.md` and `layout.json`, `narrative/` (final run), and the brief `competition/brief/Regensburg_EN_1.md` with its plans (pages as in the brief's page markers). This proposal folder has no `brief-reader/` checklist, because that skill is not written yet, so the criteria, requirements and procedure below are read from the brief itself. No results, jury report or other entry of Europan 18 were looked at.

## 1. Task and criteria

The task is a new mixed district of about 107 ha in Regensburg Nord, between the Haslbach commercial area and the existing settlements. It is to hold 2,800 to 3,400 homes for 5,000 to 6,100 residents, about 230,000 m² of commercial floor area, social infrastructure and a district centre, and to be built in sections. It should protect "the greatest possible amount of the available space resources" and treat "the high value of unsealed soil" with care (pp. 27, 30, 33). Innovative and bold ideas are expressly desired (p. 33). The session theme is Re-Sourcing.

The brief names the national jury's criteria without weighting them (p. 14). The jury therefore weights them itself, for this site:

| Criterion (brief p. 14) | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship between concept and site | carrying | The site is one of the city's last land reserves. The brief's central question is how density is distributed against new sealing (p. 33). |
| Relevance to the topic, sustainable development and adaptability | carrying | On this site, Re-Sourcing means unsealed soil as the resource. The brief requires the sealing shares as a statement (p. 34). |
| Connection between different functions | carrying | The brief's core challenge is "the interlinking of different uses" (p. 33), with flexible ground floors and no use-related blocks (p. 33). This is the interior of the district, which the jury tests hardest. |
| Relevance of the programme to the brief | contributing | The programme is fixed in detail (p. 30). Deviations can be measured and are mostly repairable. |
| Potential for integration into an urban process | contributing | The brief demands phasing, with at least 1,100 homes in the first phase (pp. 33-34). |
| Architectural and technical qualities | contributing | These are asked for at the scale of the detail areas (1:1,000 / 1:500, p. 34). |
| Innovative nature of the public spaces | supplementary | Open space ranks below the interior. Here, though, open space is the entry's idea, so how it is drawn is tested as part of the concept. |

The unwritten criteria (self-evidence, fit with the place, and whether a political majority could back it) are added in the appraisal and marked as assessments.

## 3. Rounds

The brief sets a two-stage procedure (p. 14). A local jury judges conceptual content and the degree of innovation with regard to the topic and shortlists at most 25 % of the entries. The national jury then judges the shortlist on the criteria above and makes the awards. The rounds follow that procedure.

**Round 1 (local jury): conceptual content and innovation with regard to Re-Sourcing.** The entry is eliminated in this round.

- *Own idea.* The panels present "one long green, the district gathered beside it" (panel 1). Next to the brief's framework plan (p. 37), the site plan on panel 2 repeats it almost line for line: the same residential and urban zones, the same loop street in the west and spine east of the railway, the same green belt along the settlement edge and the same green links running north. The entry's own text says so: "the design is the brief's own framework plan" (`narrative/summary.md`; `text.md`, "How it was approached"). The concept presents as its achievement a green that the framework plan already contains. The brief expressly asks for "innovative and bold ideas" (p. 33), and an own answer is missed.
- *Topic.* Reading soil as the scarce resource is right for this site (p. 33). But the one figure that would prove it, the share of sealed ground in % of the project site (p. 34), is not given. A floor area ratio of 0.45 (panel 1) and 21.7 ha of green say nothing about how much of the built half is sealed, and streets, commercial halls and courtyards are drawn in the same grey. The claim cannot be checked.
- *Contradiction in the concept.* Diagram 1 (panel 1) says the field should "let the night's cold air run south", following the north-south fresh-air corridors the brief asks to be considered (p. 33; Fig. 20, p. 31). The plan and diagram 3 then make the main green an east-west belt. The entry does not explain how the two relate.

**Round 2 (national jury): criteria.** Not reached. For the record, this is what the entry would bring into it. On the *connection between functions*, the panels show no uses on the plan at all: housing, commerce, the school, the pool and the daycares are neither labelled nor told apart on panel 2. No ground floors and no detail area are drawn. The text's claim of commercial ground floors (panel 3, "Uses") is not drawn, and so it counts as missing. On the *programme*, the deviations listed above can be measured and are partly repairable. On the *urban process*, no phasing is shown.

**Control round: can the entry be brought back?** The jury tests what speaks for it. The green is generous, more than three times the minimum. All 323 existing buildings are kept. Densities and heights stay within what the brief allows. Placing the centre at the link across the railway is a reasonable reading of the framework plan. None of these goes beyond the framework plan as the entry's own contribution, and none makes up for the missing sealing statement, the missing interior or the missing detail areas. The elimination is confirmed.

## 4. Appraisal

The entry sets the new district into the brief's framework plan for Regensburg Nord and accepts its siting as given. A western half lies between Chamer Straße and the B16, an eastern half between the railway and the forest, and the centre sits where the two halves are linked across the line (panels 2, 3; brief p. 37). The commercial halls stand at the northern edge next to Haslbach. The housing lies to the south, around a long green along the existing settlement edge. The relation to the garden-city neighbourhood in the south is handled with care: the section excerpt on panel 3 shows the new buildings meeting the houses on Haslbachweg and Chamer Straße on the real ground, and the plan keeps the existing houses inside the site. The jury acknowledges that the site is read correctly. Its limit is that the entry reads the site only through the framework plan.

The entry's strength is the consistency of its intent. The district is kept to five and six floors, with two eight-floor blocks marking the centre as the brief allows (p. 33), and the open land is held together as one network rather than broken into leftover spaces. The concept diagrams on panel 1 tell this simply and in sequence, and the title says what the project wants. As an assessment: the idea of keeping the field together would be self-evident to a lay audience and could find a political majority easily, not least because it is the city's own plan.

The doubts begin at the scale the brief asks to see. The site plan is a massing check plan: grey bars with floor numbers, all in one tone, set into the zones of the framework plan (panel 2). Nothing shows how the blocks work inside. It is not shown where the homes are entered, which ground floors open onto the street, which courtyards are private and which public, or how housing, commerce and social uses mix within a block as the brief asks (p. 33). The two detail areas, which would show exactly this, are missing, among them detail area 1 with the mobility point at the Wutzlhofen stop, which every entry had to work out (p. 34). The district centre is described as a 30 × 30 m square between the two tallest buildings (panel 3), but it is not drawn as a place, and the food market meant to anchor it (p. 33) is not placed. The commercial northern edge is made of "commercial halls of six floors" (`text.md`). The jury questions whether six-storey halls suit the small and medium firms and craft enterprises the brief asks for (p. 34). The panels must show the architecture, and the relation of the new to the existing context, three-dimensionally (p. 11). With no perspective and no isometry, neither can be judged.

The open space is the entry's idea, but as a design it fails to fully convince. The 21.7 ha are drawn as flat grey areas that coincide with the framework plan's green and green links. Their types and uses, their edges, the play areas (at least about 7,500 m², p. 28, not placed), the rainwater management areas, the retention basin that is to be integrated (p. 30) and the treatment of the biotopes are not shown. When a concept rests on its open space, a plan that does not show that space counts against the concept. Mobility is a central task of the brief (p. 34), but here it is limited to street lines: no mobility point, district garage or footpath and cycle network is drawn.

On the key figures: 2,972 homes fall within the 2,800 to 3,400 asked for (p. 33), but they are derived from an assumed 90 m² of floor area per home, and no household mix is stated, although the brief asks for one (p. 33). The number of residents is not given. At the brief's own ratio it comes to about 5,300, which sets the green requirement at about 6.4 ha and the play areas at about 8,000 m². Against that, 21.7 ha of public green is ample. Commerce reaches 215,643 m², 94 % of the 230,000 m² asked for. The shortfall follows from a project boundary that leaves out the framework plan's northern commercial land (`text.md`, note on commerce), so the boundary deviates from the brief's plan (p. 37). The pool hall reaches 90 % of its 3,000 m², and the school's sports area falls 43 m² short of its 3,000 m² minimum. The most important figure for the theme, the share of sealed ground, is missing. So are the first phase with its at least 1,100 homes and the residents per phase (pp. 33-34). Panel 1 is empty below the diagrams and panel 3 is about two-thirds empty, where these contents belong (`panel-layout/report.md`).

Overall, the entry reads the site and the theme correctly but does not go beyond the framework plan it was given. It leaves unanswered exactly the questions the brief asks at the scale of the block, the detail area and the phase. As it stands, it is a starting point for a design rather than a design.

## 5. Verdict

The entry is eliminated in the first round (conceptual content and innovation with regard to the topic) and is not shortlisted. It receives no award: neither Winner, nor Runner-up, nor Special Mention (pp. 3, 12-13, 15). The brief gives a Special Mention to an idea that is innovative though not fully suited to the site (p. 13). That is not given here, because the idea is the brief's own framework plan.

## 6. Recommendations for further work

1. Find an own idea beyond the framework plan. Decide what the entry does with the field that the city's plan does not, and let the structure plan follow from that, not the other way round.
2. State the soil sealing: fully and partly sealed areas and the degree of sealing in % of the project site, with a diagram (p. 34). Make it the entry's lead figure, since the theme rests on it.
3. Draw detail area 1, between Chamer Straße and the railway, at 1:1,000, with the mobility point at the Wutzlhofen stop, ground floors, entrances, courtyards and uses (pp. 33-34).
4. Draw detail area 2 at 1:1,000 or 1:500, preferably the district centre with its square, the two landmarks and the food market of about 1,200 m² sales area (p. 33).
5. Show the interior of the blocks: the mix of uses within each block, flexible ground floors, and the housing types and household mix (p. 33). Tell the uses apart on the site plan.
6. Show the phasing: construction phases, at least 1,100 homes in the first phase, a daycare in or near it, and the number of residents per phase (pp. 33-34).
7. Design the open space as spaces, not as zones: types and sizes, play areas at 1.5 m² per resident, the daycares' outdoor areas (950 m² each), rainwater management and the retention basin, and the biotopes (pp. 28, 30, 34). Explain how the east-west belt relates to the north-south cold-air corridors.
8. Close the programme gaps: commerce (14,357 m² missing), the pool hall (302 m² missing, with its 4,000 m² plot) and the school sports area (43 m² missing). State the number of residents.
9. Use the framework plan's project boundary (p. 37), including its northern commercial land, or justify the deviation.
10. Add the figure-ground diagram at 1:15,000, at least two perspectives, at least one isometry, and a graphic scale on every drawing (pp. 11, 34). Show the protective strips under the 150 kV line, over the main collector and along the B16 (pp. 28, 30).
11. Review the six-storey commercial halls against the brief's call for small and medium firms and craft enterprises (p. 34).
