# Jury report: "Furrows – A district combed into the field"

Europan 18, Regensburg (DE). One entry, three A1 panels (panel-layout/panel-1.png to panel-3.png). They are read against the brief (Regensburg_EN_1, PDF page numbers) and the entry's own storyline (narrative/narrative.md, narrative/text.md). The three awarded entries of this site (references/winners/, boards 1–3 each) set the standard for the level of representation. Statements marked *(assessment)* are the jury's judgement, not a measurement.

## 1. Task and criteria

The city asks for ideas for a new district of about 107 ha on farmland 4 km north of the centre (p. 27). The site lies between the B16, the garden city and the Haslbach commercial area, and is cut by the railway and Pilsenallee.

The district is to hold:
- 2,800 to 3,400 homes for 5,000 to 6,100 residents
- 230,000 m² of commerce for small firms and crafts
- a district centre at a new Wutzlhofen stop, with a mobility point and a 6-platform bus station
- three daycares, a primary school, the Nordbad indoor pool and a food market (pp. 30, 33–34)

The brief also asks for little sealing, for the fresh-air corridors to be respected and for few cars. The district is to be built in sections, phase 1 between Chamer Straße and the railway with at least 1,100 homes (pp. 28, 33).

The brief's criteria (p. 14) are not weighted. The jury weights them as follows:

| Criterion (p. 14) | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship between concept and site | carrying | An ideas competition on open farmland stands or falls by how the idea reads the place: air, soil, edges, rail. |
| Relevance of the programme to the brief | carrying | The city needs a district it can zone. The numbers on p. 30 are minimum and/or maximum values (p. 28). |
| Connection between different functions, i.e. how blocks and uses are organised | carrying | The jury tests every entry hardest on its interior. At this scale that means the organisation of blocks, ground floors and homes. |
| Innovative nature of the public spaces | carrying | The entry makes open space its idea, so it is judged on it at full weight. |
| Sustainable development and adaptability (topic "Re-Sourcing") | contributing | Underlies the whole brief, but is largely covered by the open-space and sealing questions above. |
| Integration into an urban process (phasing) | contributing | Required by pp. 33–34, but secondary to the figure itself. |
| Architectural and technical qualities | supplementary | At ideas level they are expected as typologies, not as buildings. |

The jury also weighs three unwritten criteria: self-evidence, fit with the place, and whether a political majority could back the scheme.

## 3. Rounds

The rounds follow the procedure of p. 14.

**Round 1, compliance with the rules: passed.** Format, code box, numbering, language and the required drawings are present. The missing housing mix and the cropped site plan are noted but do not exclude the entry.

**Round 1, concept and innovation against "Re-Sourcing": passed.** The idea is stated in one sentence and drawn in six steps on panel 1:
- strips along the cold air;
- an unsealed public furrow between them;
- the furrow planted before the strips beside it are built;
- unbuilt land farmed in the meantime.

It treats soil, air and the farmland's history as the resources to keep. The entry is placed among the shortlisted quarter.

**Round 2, concept and site: passed.** The north–south comb follows the cold air towards the garden city (panel 1, climate and fresh-air diagrams). The working heads facing the rail and the B16 answer the noise demand of pp. 28 and 33.

**Round 2, programme and the organisation of blocks and uses: eliminated here.** The totals fit the brief's ranges, but play area, the school's sports area, the food market and commerce fall short. The entry's own sealing and green figures contradict each other.

The interior decides it:
- No housing mix is shown (p. 33).
- There is no dwelling plan, floor plan or section through a building.
- The six building types on panel 3 are volumes with floor areas and tree counts; their use reads only "housing" or "commerce".
- Ground floors appear only as hatching in detail area 1. Detail area 2 shows one block and a row of town houses with nothing inside.

The panels don't show how 3,016 homes and 226,750 m² of commerce are organised, or how the flexible ground floors of p. 33 would work.

**Round 2, public space (noted, not a separate hurdle).** The furrows are the entry's idea. On the site plan they are pink outlines laid over trees and rain pools, not designed spaces, and no drawing shows their paths, edges or uses at 1:1,000. Where open space is the idea, this counts against the concept.

**Control round.** The jury tested bringing the entry back as a Special Mention, given to an entry that is "innovative, yet not sufficiently suitable for the site" (p. 14, § 10.4). It does not qualify. Its strength is a clear, well-placed principle, not an innovation beyond the green spines the awarded entries already offer. Its weakness lies in the very open space it promises. The elimination is confirmed.

## 4. Appraisal

The entry lays the new district across the farmland as a comb of north–south strips, aligned with the cold air that flows towards the garden city. Between the strips it keeps six unsealed public furrows, at least 40 m wide, running from Haslbach to the garden city.

The siting is persuasive in its logic. The strips turn the climate requirement of p. 33 into the figure itself. The working heads along the railway and the B16 screen the homes behind them. The district centre sits where the strips meet the new Wutzlhofen stop. The six diagrams on panel 1 state this with a clarity the jury values: the idea is self-evident and could be explained to a citizens' meeting in one sentence *(assessment)*.

The long section and the climate diagrams support the reading of a district that steps down towards the garden city and leaves the air paths open. The phasing is well considered. Phase 1 lies between Chamer Straße and the railway, as the brief asks, and carries the stop, the bus station, the pool, the school and two daycares; each phase is said to be connected on completion. Planting the furrow before its strips and farming the unbuilt land in the meantime is a convincing answer to the session theme. A council could support it, since it delivers green before density *(assessment)*.

Detail area 1 is the entry's most informative drawing. It shows Station Square with the market hall, the six-bay bus station, the district garage, the pool and the food market, with entrances and public ground floors marked; here the centre becomes legible. The 9,600 m² district garage directly on the square is questioned in a district meant for few cars (pp. 27, 34). In the perspective, the large single-storey market hall reads as a long closed shed rather than a centre.

The key figures fit the brief only at first sight. 3,016 homes for 5,387 residents lie within 2,800–3,400. Phase 1 reaches 1,123 homes, 23 over the minimum, on an assumed 95 m² per home. Commerce stays 3,250 m² below the framework value. Public green is ample against the 6.5 ha required, yet the entry gives it as 14.0, 18.3, 31.1 and 48.6 ha in different places. Play area is 1,034 m² short against the entry's own resident count, and the school's 3,000 m² sports area is missing. The food market is labelled 1,200 m², while the text admits 893 m². Sealing is 37.2 % fully sealed on the panels but 30.9 % in the text; the jury cannot tell which holds, and the panel figure exceeds the entry's own 35 % ceiling. The site measured is 112.8 ha, not the brief's 107 ha.

The doubts concentrate on the interior. The building types (rows, L blocks, courtyard ensembles, town houses, halls and U blocks) are volumes at 1:1,000 with roofs and tree counts. They have no plans, no household mix from cluster housing to one-room flats (p. 33), and no ground floors that would make the strips mixed rather than zoned. Lining the heads with halls and U blocks for "commerce" comes close to the use-related block formation p. 33 asks to avoid. The awarded entries show their housing models, ground-floor uses and inhabited courtyards; against them this entry stays at the level of a massing study.

The furrows themselves, the idea of the entry, fail to fully convince on the plans. On the 1:3,000 site plan they are outlines laid over trees and pools, and no drawing shows a furrow at 1:1,000 with its path, edges, rain pools and the gates from the strips. The site plan is cropped and loses the northern tip of the site. The isometric at 1:5,000 is an overview with no close-up of the centre. The perspectives are sparse and do not yet show the life the text promises.

Overall, the entry offers a clear, well-placed principle that reads the site's air and soil and could gather political support. It does not yet show that the comb holds a mixed, inhabited district inside its strips, nor that its furrows are the public spaces they are meant to be.

## 5. Verdict

Shortlisted in round 1; eliminated in round 2 on the organisation of blocks and uses. No award: neither Winner, Runner-up nor Special Mention.

## 6. Recommendations for further work

1. Draw one furrow at 1:1,000 (or 1:500) as detail area 2: path, rain pools, planting, edges, the gates from the strips and what happens in it.
2. Show the interior: a housing mix from cluster housing to one-room flats and homes for 4+ people (p. 33), typical floor and ground-floor plans for at least the row and the courtyard ensemble, and ground-floor uses across the whole site plan.
3. Mix the heads: put homes or workshops above the commerce where noise allows, to avoid use-related blocks (p. 33).
4. Reconcile the numbers in one table: one sealing figure (fully, partly, unsealed, on the brief's 107 ha), one public-green figure, and homes from a stated flat size.
5. Close the programme gaps: play area to at least 8,100 m² (1.5 m² × residents), the school's 3,000 m² sports area and 2,000 m² yard, a food market of 1,200 m² in fact, and commerce up to 230,000 m².
6. Remove the Chamer Straße–Sandgasse car link the phasing finds at completion, and reconsider the size of the district garage at the stop.
7. Draw and dimension the canal, the 150 kV strip and the B16 setback on the site plan.
8. Show the whole site on the site plan, uncropped, and add a close-up isometric of Station Square.
9. Give the perspectives programme and life: who uses the furrow and the square, and what the ground floors hold.
10. Add a graphic scale, or a "not to scale" note, to every graphic, and produce the A4 text PDF and the three communication images.
