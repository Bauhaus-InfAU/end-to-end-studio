# Jury report: "Long Fields – Living in rows between kept fields"

Europan 18, Regensburg (DE), one entry judged alone. Panels: `panel-layout/panel-1.png` to `panel-3.png` (P1–P3). Brief: `competition/brief/Regensburg_EN_1.md`, PDF pages. Text: `narrative/text.md`, `narrative/communication.md`. References: the three awarded entries of this site in `competition/references/winners/`.

## 1. Task and criteria

The task is a new district of about 107 ha on the last large field in the north of Regensburg, about 4 km from the centre (p. 27): 2,800 to 3,400 homes for about 5,000 to 6,100 residents, at least 1,100 of them in the first construction phase between Chamer Straße and the railway, with an intermodal mobility point at the new Wutzlhofen stop, a district centre, three daycares, a primary school, the Nordbad pool and commerce for small firms, planned with few to no cars, little sealing and respect for the fresh-air corridors (pp. 30, 33–34).

The brief names its criteria for the second round without weighting them (p. 14). The jury weights them as follows:

- **Carrying:** the relationship between concept and site, and the relevance of the programme to the brief. On an open field at this scale a district is accepted or rejected by whether its figure belongs to the place and whether it delivers the homes, phase 1 and the social infrastructure the city must account for (p. 33, "must").
- **Carrying:** the connection between functions and the innovative nature of the public spaces. The jury tests every entry hardest on how its blocks and uses are organised inside, and this entry makes its open space the idea.
- **Contributing:** relevance to the topic (sustainable development, adaptability), read through sealing, climate and the kept land; and the potential for integration into an urban process, read through the phasing.
- **Supplementary:** architectural and technical qualities, which an ideas competition at 1:3,000 can show only in outline.

The jury also judges three unwritten criteria, marked as assessments: self-evidence of the idea, fit with the place, and whether a political majority in the city could back it.

## 3. Rounds

**Round 1, compliance (p. 14).** Passed with reservations. Format, anonymity and the required drawings are in place; the phase-1 shortfall, the missing housing mix and the contradictory sealing figure are recorded.

**Round 1, conceptual content and innovation against the topic (p. 14).** Passed. The idea, twelve north–south rows with six field strips of 45 m kept open between them, can be said in one sentence and reads in the site plan (P1) and the pictograms 1–5 (P1). It answers Re-Sourcing with soil and cold air as resources.

**Round 2, concept and site; programme (p. 14).** Eliminated here. The kept fields that give the entry its title are drawn as wooded parks and rain pools, not as fields (P1 site plan, P2 isometric); the entry's own analysis records farmland falling from 70 to 11 ha (`narrative/summary.md`). The figure therefore keeps the grain of the field but not the field. The programme is short where the city is firmest: 942 instead of at least 1,100 homes in phase 1 (P2), no housing mix, no school sports ground. The interior of the blocks, where the "two doors" are meant to work, is not shown.

**Control round.** The jury tested bringing the entry back on the strength of its clear figure, its climate reasoning (P1 climate rules) and its legible phasing (P2). It does not come back: the phase-1 gap is a fixed requirement, the sealing figure is contradicted between panel and text, and the representation stays well below the awarded boards of this site (closeness 34 of 65, 2.6 of 5 on average; detail area 2, the perspectives and the building types at 2). A Special Mention, reserved for innovative ideas not fully adapted to the site (p. 14), was considered; the idea of rows in strips is consistent but not new enough to carry it alone.

## 4. Appraisal

The district is laid across the field in twelve rows that run north–south with the long farm parcels and the night-time cold-air flow, and six strips of 45 m stay open between them (P1). This siting is the entry's strongest decision. It reads the place from its own traces rather than importing a figure, it lets the air pass towards the city, and it places the taller rows along the railway and Pilsenallee as a noise edge, as the brief asks (p. 28, p. 33). The climate-rules map and the pictograms on P1 build the argument step by step, and the main centre at the new Wutzlhofen stop, with the bus station of six bays, the market hall, the district garage and 400 bike spaces, is placed where the brief wants it (P3 detail area 1; p. 27). The kept basin, biotopes and compensation land are woven into the strips, and all existing buildings are kept.

The doubts begin where the idea should become a place. The strips are drawn as groves, parks and rain pools, so the "kept fields" become a park system much like any green-belt scheme; the farming that the text and the phasing promise ("its land stays farmed", P2) is not visible in any drawing. The rows themselves are repeated pairs of bars of four to six storeys with little variation; between them the lanes are drawn as ochre bands, but courtyards, private gardens, play and the threshold between lane and strip are not drawn in either detail area. The promise that every home has a door to the lane and one to the strip is not legible in the plans; detail area 2, titled "the doorstep", shows seven bars among trees and no use, garden or play area at all (P3). How the blocks are organised inside, which ground floors hold the commerce of 240,000 m², and where the many small firms the brief wants would work (p. 34) is answered only by four labels in detail area 1. This is missed.

The key figures are mixed. The 3,269 homes for 5,838 residents sit inside the brief's range, and the 28.6 ha of public green is four times the minimum of about 7 ha (P2). The first phase, however, holds 942 homes against the 1,100 the city must account for (p. 33), 14 % short, and its 1,683 residents rest on the densest rows. Sealing is given as 44.9 % on P2 but as 56.6 % in the entry's text and analysis; either figure doubles today's 22.5 % and sits uneasily with an entry whose idea is to keep the soil. Play at 7,929 m² falls 828 m² short of 1.5 m² for the planned residents, and the daycare shown has 320 m² of outdoor ground where 950 m² are required. Public green appears as 28.6, 31 and 31.3 ha in different documents. The jury questions whether the numbers have been reconciled at all.

On the unwritten criteria the jury's assessment is divided. The idea is self-evident and fits the place; a council could explain it in one sentence. Whether a majority would back it is doubtful as long as phase 1 does not deliver the required homes and the field the title promises is not shown to survive. The panels add to this: the perspectives are empty streets with standing pictogram figures, one titled "At the station" shows no station, and the building types are cut off at the panel edge with technical notes showing (P3).

Overall, "Long Fields" offers a clear and site-specific figure with a sound climate logic, but it fails to fully convince as a district: the field it keeps is not drawn as a field, the inside of its rows is not designed, and it misses the brief's firmest number for the first phase.

## 5. Verdict

No award (neither Winner, Runner-up nor Special Mention). The entry is eliminated in the second round; the control round confirms it.

## 6. Recommendations for further work

1. Raise phase 1 to at least 1,100 homes (p. 33), for example by adding a row or a storey near Station square, and restate the residents per phase.
2. Reconcile the sealing figure (44.9 % on P2, 56.6 % in the text) into one, show fully and partly sealed as a diagram for today and the design, and reduce it towards the entry's own aim.
3. Draw the strips as what the title promises: fields, allotments and meadows with their use and who farms them, not as wooded parks; show which strips are farmed until built.
4. Design the interior of one row at 1:500 or 1:1,000: lane, entrances on both sides, gardens, shared yards, play, the threshold to the strip, and ground-floor uses.
5. Redraw detail area 2 with uses, play and gardens labelled, or move it to the Field school with the school, the pool and the 3,000 m² sports ground (pp. 30, 34).
6. State the housing mix (p. 33), from cluster and cross-generational housing to one-room flats and large homes, and tie it to named building types with one line each on who lives there.
7. Give each daycare at least 950 m² of outdoor play and bring play to 1.5 m² per planned resident.
8. Show that no street joins Chamer Straße and Sandgasse directly, and draw the B16 20 m distance, the canal and the 150 kV strip (pp. 28, 30).
9. Write the Study Site measures and the construction methods (p. 34), and supply the three communication JPGs (p. 12).
10. Redraw the perspectives with programme, people in action and a foreground, one of them actually at the station, and fit the building types whole into their frame.
