# Gaps and their causes

Steps 1 and 2 of the review of 04.11.2026. I compared the winners' nine boards (`competition/references/winners/*/board-N.jpg` and the drawings cut from them) with our run-10 panels, plus runs 8 and 9 where they help. Then I traced each gap to the skill, script or harness rule that causes it. I didn't open `competition/feedback/`, any run's `jury/`, `docs/`, `plans/` or the environment folder.

The sheets in `evidence/` put each kind of drawing side by side: the winners on top, ours below. They hold cuts of the winners' boards, which are other people's work, so `evidence/` stays out of git (see `.gitignore` here).

One fact frames everything below. No run so far had a reference image. The `version.md` of runs 8, 9 and 10 says "Reference images: none. `competition/references/` holds only its README". So the gap isn't the result of skills failing to copy the winners. They never saw them. The real question is what happens on run 11, when the winners are there. My answer, from the code, is that run 11 changes very little, because only one skill looks at references and the scripts can't take in what it would learn (cause K1 and K2).

## Gaps by kind of drawing

Gap ids (G1 to G11) are what `changes.md` and the judges refer to.

### G1. Site plan, 1:3,000 (`evidence/02_site-plan_same-size.jpg`, a 150 mm patch of each board at the same print size)

The winners draw a lived-in city. Buildings are white or pale with a thin dark outline and a cast shadow, and each carries its storeys as a Roman numeral. The blocks vary: L, U, open and closed courtyards, town-house rows, point buildings and a tower at the station. Trees are everywhere. They line streets as avenues, stand in courtyards, cluster in parks and fill the kept woods as canopy, and the gardens of the existing city get trees too. The accent colour (rose in winners 1 and 3, warm pink in winner 2) marks public ground: squares, shared streets, the centre, the hub. Courtyards have a semi-private tone. Uses are written in place (elementary school, kindergarten, café, supermarket, central mobility hub). Fields have stripes and woods have texture.

Ours draws a diagram of slabs. All 141 new buildings are red rectangles about 13 m by 30 to 50 m, mostly parallel, with Arabic storey numbers in white. The plan has no shadows. It has no trees at all at 1:3,000, because only the detail areas get dots. Streets are grey bands with turning heads, there is no public ground in an accent, and no use or place is named. The accent sits on the buildings, so the eye reads "141 red boxes" rather than "a district with a centre".

### G2. Detail areas, 1:1,000 and 1:500 (`evidence/03_detail-areas.jpg`)

The winners' detail areas carry a new layer of design. They show ground-floor plans with uses and entrances (post office, savings bank, bus parking, bike garage), apartment layouts at 1:500 (winner 1), uses with their areas ("library 2,900 sqm", winner 3), paving, bike stands, bus bays, sports fields, playground equipment, PV on roofs, private gardens and community gardens.

Ours is the site plan enlarged. The same red boxes and numbers, street-tree dots and flat green fill come out three times bigger. Nothing new appears at the larger scale, and that is the "interior" a jury tests hardest.

### G3. Building types (`evidence/04_building-types.jpg`)

The winners show ensembles: a courtyard of several buildings with its yard, trees, entries, roofs (gable, green, PV) and a façade rhythm, on a piece of street. Leader-line notes say what each one offers ("shared and multiused rooms", "entry passages to multiple directions"). Winner 2 draws windows and gables, and winner 3 draws each type in its context as a round vignette.

Ours draws one extruded box per type: one face red, floor lines, no windows, no roof, no context and no trees. Under each box sit data lines (footprint, floors, floor area).

### G4. Isometric (`evidence/05_isometric.jpg`)

The winners make it large. Winner 2 draws it at 1:1,000 over half a board and winner 3 at 1:1,500 over two thirds. It is zoomed on the centre or covers the whole district. Volumes are white with shaded sides, there are thousands of trees, and the accent sits on public ground. Labels name the uses (mobility hub, supermarket, library, café). Birds, people and tents bring it to life, and the edges fade into white.

Ours is 144 by 144 mm at 1:10,000: red boxes on straw-yellow fields, with no trees and no labels.

### G5. Perspectives (`evidence/06_perspectives.jpg`)

The winners illustrate. Winner 3 uses line drawing with flat colour, winner 1 vector collage and winner 2 watercolour collage, each in the board's palette. Every view shows a scene: people doing what the story says, and the hub's programme written on its façade ("sky bar", "co-working", "WUTZLHUB", "supermarket").

Ours are photoreal Gemini renders of grey concrete boxes with paving and a few people. The image model added things the design doesn't hold (a tractor, a field robot, crenellations in run 8, domes in run 9). They look like a different project from the panels around them.

### G6. Concept diagrams and phases (`evidence/07_concept-diagrams.jpg`)

The winners use metaphor and people: "status symbol of the individual → identification for the community", the old town's towers carried onto the site. Winner 1 builds the idea up in layers (green connector + community squares + residential streets + courtyards), winner 1's activity pictograms show people, and winner 2's forest belt is drawn as a ring with icons. The phases come as a numbered map with the citizens each phase brings.

Ours are six plan outlines of the site with hatch and a red fill, all in the same frame, with no people, no metaphor and no 3D. Worse, they were drawn before the structure plan, so they show a 75.6 ha field against the plan's 41.1 ha (run 10). In run 9 they put phase 1 on the wrong side of the railway.

### G7. Sections (`evidence/08_sections.jpg`)

The winners' sections are story strips. Ground bands are coloured by zone (B16, noise protection, neighbourhood, Chamer Straße, supermarket), arcs and dots above the line name a theme at its place ("insect hotel", "solidarity agriculture", "cold air corridor"), and trees and people stand along it. It is long and thin and easy to read.

Ours is a true-scale terrain profile, nearly flat at 1:3,000, with grey ground, tiny buildings and lollipop trees in the 1:500 excerpt. Run 10's panels left it out because its 564 by 36 mm frame was too flat.

### G8. Thematic plans: mobility, open space, climate, phases (`evidence/09_thematic-plans.jpg`)

The winners make a set. Winner 3's board 2 has four plans of the same size in one row (mobility, phases with typology, blue-green, energy), each on the same pale base, with icons and short labels. Winner 1's mobility concept uses one colour per mode and an icon legend, and winner 2 shows mobilities and communities as twin diagrams.

Each of our skills draws its own map at its own scale with its own legend. The mobility plan is 114 mm wide at 1:15,000, and the climate rules map carries a legend column of paragraphs.

### G9. The panels as a whole (`evidence/01_boards.jpg`)

The winners' boards have one hero drawing that fills the board's width: the site plan on board 1, a detail area or the isometric on boards 2 and 3. Above it runs a band of diagrams, below it a band with the section. The boards are dense to the edge. The title is a graphic element, and key numbers are big type ("7.010 people", "1/3 sealed", "4,2 km forest trail", "9,410 citizens").

Ours put drawings of mismatched sizes into a flowing grid, with white gaps between them and number tables with a source column ("The field and the sealed ground, counted", two versions of the sealing and of homes per phase). The section is missing, the isometric is small and detail area 2 is cropped by 52 %.

### G10. The urban design itself

The drawings can't beat the geometry under them. The winners' plans hold a clear figure (a green connector, a star-shaped park, a forest belt) with designed centres, squares at the stops, a hub building with its tower, and three or four block types mixed in each neighbourhood. Ours holds combs of parallel bars at one bearing (8°) in every neighbourhood. The centres are a 30 m square, and no hub building exists ("no hub building in the massing", run-10 mobility summary).

### G11. Things drawn before what they show

The pictograms came before the structure plan (G6). The panels print homes per phase in two versions because massing and structure plan disagree (run-10 panel 3). Isometric, plans, mobility and section were each drawn at a size other than their frame (run-10 log, 00:08 to 00:09).
